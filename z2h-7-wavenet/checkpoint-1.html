Checkpoint 1: a wider window, a tree, and a fair fight

Ties together just widen the window, WaveNet's tree idea, batched matmul and FlattenConsecutive, the silent BatchNorm bug, scaling up to 76,579 params and convolutions and the workflow, with matmul shapes, view, broadcasting and BatchNorm from earlier courses. Lecture 6, 0:17:11 to 0:55:26.

What this ties together, and why now. The core of Lecture 6 is one experiment told in six cards: give the model more context, then decide how that context gets mixed. The flat MLP mixes all 8 characters in one layer. The tree mixes them two at a time, and batched @ plus a free view is all it takes to build. A 3-D tensor then breaks BatchNorm without an error. Scaling the tree up gets val below 2.0, and a convolution is the same tree computed faster. The next course throws the tree away for self-attention, so check now that you can tell which idea bought which part of the gain: 2.1065 → 2.0342 → 2.0202 → 1.9937.

Comes back from earlier courses: the batched @ is matmul by shapes from the toolkit. FlattenConsecutive is view and storage from z2h-4. The silent BatchNorm bug is the same kind of accident as the broadcasting keepdim trap in z2h-3. Per-channel statistics and running buffers are BatchNorm forward and BatchNorm at inference from z2h-5. Reading the train–val gap is splits and fit.

How to use this page. Close the cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before you open its Check. The questions are mixed on purpose and don't say which card they come from, so part of the work is choosing the idea. If an answer is wrong or shaky, its Check names the exact section to reopen.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. The final model has n_embd 24 and n_hidden 128. A batch of 32 contexts comes out of the Embedding as (32, 8, 24). Write the shape after every FlattenConsecutive(2) and every Linear, down to the logits. Then explain why Linear(48, 128) accepts a 3-D input at all.
    Check
    • (32, 4, 48) → Linear → (32, 4, 128) → (32, 2, 256) → Linear → (32, 2, 128) → (32, 256), squeezed because one group is left → Linear → (32, 128) → Linear → (32, 27)
    • each FlattenConsecutive(2) halves the middle number (positions) and doubles the last (channels): 8 → 4 → 2 → 1 nodes, 24 → 48 and 128 → 256 numbers per node
    • @ only multiplies along the last dimension of its left input; the leading (32, 4) is treated as 128 separate rows, each pushed through the same (48, 128) weight
    • level 1's weight is 48 × 128 = 6,144, matching the scale-up card's table

    If you missed it, reopen batched matmul and FlattenConsecutive, "Only the last dimension gets multiplied" and "Grouping pairs is a free view", and the toolkit's matmul by shapes, "More than two dimensions, and counting parameters".

  2. which idea?Your first tree net uses n_hidden 200, as the flat MLP did, and it beats the flat block_size-8 net on val. You post the result. A reviewer says the comparison proves nothing. Which idea is the reviewer using, what would you change, and what happens when you change it?
    Check
    • compare architectures at equal parameter counts: the n_hidden-200 tree has 170,897 parameters, against the flat net's 22,097, about 7.7× (170,897 / 22,097 = 7.73), so a win could be size alone
    • shrink n_hidden to 68: 270 + (1,360 + 136) + 2 × (9,248 + 136) + 1,863 = 22,397
    • at equal size the first run ties: val 2.029 against flat's 2.027 in the notebook. The hierarchy alone bought nothing yet

    If you missed it, reopen batched matmul and FlattenConsecutive, "A fair fight needs equal parameters", and WaveNet's tree idea, "Same budget, different wiring".

  3. You change block_size from 3 to 8 and rebuild the dataset. How many training rows are there now, and what shape is Xtr? Then give the concrete reason 8 characters help on this data, and say what the train–val column tells you about the wider flat net.
    Check
    • still 182,625 rows: one row per character to predict. Only the width changes, (182625, 3) → (182625, 8)
    • the training names average 6.1 letters: only 2.0% fit in a 3-character window, 94.7% fit in 8, so the model finally sees the whole name when it decides where it ends
    • val falls 2.1065 → 2.0342, but val − train grows from 0.048 to 0.118: the one wide first layer fits the training names better than it generalises
    • the only parameters added are in the first layer: 10 × (8 − 3) × 200 = 10,000, from 12,097 to 22,097

    If you missed it, reopen just widen the window, "What the rows look like now" and "The result", and z2h-4's rolling-window dataset, "Counting the rows".

  4. debugYour 22,397-parameter tree net trains 200k steps with no error and no warning. Val comes out at 2.0265, no better than the flat net. You print model.layers[3].running_mean.shape and get (1, 4, 68). It was created as torch.zeros(68). What is wrong, why did nothing crash, and what should val be after the fix?
    Check
    • BatchNorm gets (32, 4, 68) but reduces over dimension 0 only, so it keeps a separate mean and variance for each of the 4 positions: 4 × 68 = 272 statistics, each from 32 numbers
    • no crash because broadcasting makes it fit: 0.9 · (68,) + 0.1 · (1, 4, 68) is a legal (1, 4, 68). It's the same kind of accident as the z2h-3 keepdim trap, where a square table let the wrong axis fit
    • fix: for 3-D input, reduce over (0, 1), which gives (1, 1, 68), one statistic per channel from 32 × 4 = 128 numbers. The same Linear made every position, so one channel is one feature
    • val 2.0265 → 2.0202 on your M3 (notebook 2.029 → 2.022), a gain of 0.0063 from one run each

    If you missed it, reopen the silent BatchNorm bug, "What BatchNorm sees inside the tree" and "The shapes, before and after the fix"; z2h-5's BatchNorm forward, "The formula, one column at a time"; and z2h-3's keepdim trap, "Why did the bug look so plausible?".

  5. You want 16 characters of context instead of 8. What does it cost the flat net (n_embd 10, n_hidden 200) and the tree (n_embd 10, n_hidden 68) in parameters? Why is the tree's price so much smaller?
    Check
    • flat: the first layer goes from 80 × 200 = 16,000 to 160 × 200 = 32,000 weights, +16,000 (38,097 in total), and 80% of the window would be '.' padding, because the longest name has 15 letters
    • tree: one more level, 136 × 68 + 136 = 9,384 parameters (weights plus BatchNorm's γ, β)
    • the tree's depth grows with log₂ of the context, and each level uses one shared weight for every pair, so the pair count doesn't enter the parameter count. The flat layer's size grows in step with the window

    If you missed it, reopen WaveNet's tree idea, "Why it beats the alternatives", and just widen the window, the "Widen to 16" cell.

  6. which idea?A friend rewrites the scaled-up tree as a stack of dilated causal convolutions, keeps the makemore training loop (32 random rows per step) and expects val to drop below 1.9937. What will happen to val, and what will actually change? When would the rewrite pay off?
    Check
    • val won't change for that reason: the convolution computes exactly the same function with the same weights. Karpathy calls convolutions "strictly for efficiency" (0:47:44)
    • what changes is cost: for the 8 rows of "diondre" a loop builds 56 tree nodes, of which only 34 are distinct. A convolution computes each of the 34 once (7R vs 3R + 10 nodes for R rows)
    • it pays off only when many outputs come from the same sequence. 32 random rows almost never share a name, so there is nothing to reuse (the card's inference, not measured in the lecture)
    • "causal": a node only sees characters to its left; "dilated": level k's inputs are 1, 2, 4 positions apart

    If you missed it, reopen convolutions and the workflow, "Where the eight trees overlap" and "So should you rewrite your model as a convolution?".

  7. Why is e.view(B, 4, 20) on an embedding output of shape (B, 8, 10) the same as gluing each even position to the next odd one, and why does it cost nothing? What happens with FlattenConsecutive(3) on 8 positions, and why couldn't a view pair position 0 with position 2?
    Check
    • one example's 8 embeddings sit one after another in memory, 80 numbers. Reading them as 4 rows of 20 puts characters 0+1, 2+3, 4+5, 6+7 in each row. The lab checks it equals torch.cat([e[:, ::2], e[:, 1::2]], 2): True
    • a view only changes the strides (how many slots to skip per step), never the storage, so no numbers are copied
    • 8 isn't divisible by 3: shape '[4, 2, 30]' is invalid for input of size 320. It fails loudly, which is why Karpathy writes out B, T, C instead of −1
    • positions 0 and 2 aren't next to each other in memory, so a skipping ("dilated") pairing needs a permute first

    If you missed it, reopen batched matmul and FlattenConsecutive, "Grouping pairs is a free view" and "When the view trick doesn't fit", and z2h-4's view and storage, "One strip of memory, read as many shapes".

  8. which idea?After the scale-up, a teammate writes in the report: "the WaveNet tree beats the flat MLP, 1.9937 vs 2.0342". Train went 1.9110 → 1.7690 and val 2.0202 → 1.9937 between the 22K and 76K tree. Which two ideas from this course undercut the claim, and what would you run before believing it?
    Check
    • equal parameters: 76,579 is 3.4× the 22,397 tree and the 22,097 flat net, and the flat net was never scaled to 76K. Karpathy lists a big flat net as a thing to try and says it "would be kind of embarrassing" if it won (0:55:26)
    • one seed and no harness: every row of the performance log is a single run, so small steps like the BatchNorm fix's 0.0063 may be noise. Karpathy calls his own process "guessing and checking" (0:46:58)
    • the gap: train fell by 0.142 and val by only 0.0265, so val − train doubled from 0.109 to 0.225. More width is starting to buy memorisation
    • run: a flat net at about 76K parameters, both models over several seeds, compared on val only (keep test untouched)

    If you missed it, reopen scaling up, "The whole lecture in five rows" and "Did the tree win, or the parameters?"; convolutions and the workflow, "How the work actually happens"; and z2h-4's splits and fit, "Reading the two losses".
