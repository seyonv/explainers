Checkpoint 3: the trial family

Ties together reading an MGF, binomial and beta-binomial, Poisson, geometric, hypergeometric and negative binomial. They are six counts built from yes/no trials, and choosing between them comes down to three questions: what is counted, is anything replaced, and is the rate fixed?

How to use this page. Close the six cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before opening its Check. A wrong or shaky answer isn't a failure: it tells you exactly which section to reopen. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you first have to work out which distribution applies.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which concept?Defects per wafer have a sample mean of 2 and a variance of 5. Which distribution would you fit, and with what parameters?
    Check
    • variance above the mean rules out the Poisson (equal) and the binomial (below): this is over-dispersion
    • a negative binomial: p = mean/variance = 2/5, r = μp/q = 2 × 0.4/0.6 = 4/3
    • check: μ + μ²/r = 2 + 4 × 3/4 = 5 ✓; r need not be a whole number in the gamma-mixture reading

    Missed it? Reopen negative binomial, "The over-dispersed Poisson, and what r does".

  2. which concept?Deal 5 cards from a shuffled deck and count the hearts. Name the distribution, and give its mean and variance next to the binomial's.
    Check
    • hypergeometric: N = 52, K = 13, n = 5, drawn without replacement
    • mean 5 × 13/52 = 1.25, the same as Bin(5, ¼)
    • variance 5 × ¼ × ¾ × 47/51 = 235/272 ≈ 0.864, against the binomial's 0.9375: the finite-population correction

    Missed it? Reopen hypergeometric, "The model".

  3. A fair coin has come up tails 10 times in a row. What is the probability of no head in the next 3 flips, and which property gives it in one line?
    Check
    • memorylessness of the geometric wait: P(X > 13 | X > 10) = P(X > 3)
    • = (1/2)³ = 1/8
    • the 10 tails change nothing, because the tail is a pure power of q

    Missed it? Reopen geometric, "Memoryless, proven in one line".

  4. M(t) = (0.5 + 0.5eᵗ)¹⁰. Name the distribution and give its mean and variance.
    Check
    • it matches (q + peᵗ)ⁿ: Bin(10, ½)
    • M′(0) = np = 5, and Var = npq = 2.5
    • the match is a proof, because an MGF that is finite near 0 determines the distribution

    Missed it? Reopen reading an MGF, "The four readings".

  5. Bin(1000, 0.002) against Poisson(2): P(0) for each, and why are they so close?
    Check
    • binomial: 0.998¹⁰⁰⁰ ≈ 0.13506; Poisson: e−2 ≈ 0.13534
    • many trials, each unlikely, with a fixed mean np = 2: the rare-event limit
    • the error is at most λ²/n = 0.004 in total variation (Le Cam)

    Missed it? Reopen Poisson, "Where it comes from".

  6. which concept?Twenty schools each report how many of 10 students passed. The counts are far more spread out than Bin(10, p̄) allows. Which model, and what is it assuming about the schools?
    Check
    • the beta-binomial: each school has its own pass rate, drawn from a Beta
    • students are independent within a school, given its rate
    • the variance picks up the factor (α + β + n)/(α + β + 1); the mean stays at np̄

    Missed it? Reopen binomial and beta-binomial, the worked example.

  7. Calls arrive on two independent lines at 2 and 3 per hour. What is the distribution of the total calls in 2 hours, and P(no calls at all)?
    Check
    • independent Poissons add: 2 × 2 + 2 × 3 = Poisson(10)
    • P(0) = e−10 ≈ 4.5 × 10−5
    • the MGFs multiply and the exponents add; independence is what makes the total Poisson

    Missed it? Reopen Poisson, problem 1.

  8. Why is NB(5, 0.7) not memoryless, when each of its five pieces is?
    Check
    • P(X ≥ 1) = 0.8319, but P(X ≥ 2 | X ≥ 1) = 0.697
    • a count that has already reached 1 may be partway through a geometric piece, so the chance of continuing depends on the past
    • only r = 1, the geometric itself, has tails that are a pure power

    Missed it? Reopen geometric, "How it differs from related ideas".

3 · Project: which count model fits?

What it reinforces: naming a distribution from its MGF (MGF), the sum-of-geometrics and gamma-Poisson readings (negative binomial), memorylessness as a test (geometric), variance = mean as a test (Poisson), and overdispersion from a varying rate (beta-binomial).

You are handed 10⁵ claim counts and must choose a model. Make the data with np.random.default_rng(0).negative_binomial(5, 0.7, 10**5), but pretend you don't know where it came from. In about 30 lines of Python with numpy:

  1. Dispersion test. Compute the sample mean and variance. Which of binomial, Poisson and negative binomial are still possible?
  2. Fit two models. Poisson with λ = mean; NB by moments, p̂ = mean/variance and r̂ = mean·p̂/(1 − p̂).
  3. Compare where it matters. The data's P(0) and P(X ≥ 6) against each fitted model. Which model underrates the heavy claimers, and by how much?
  4. Memoryless test. Compute the data's P(X ≥ 1) and P(X ≥ 2 | X ≥ 1). Is the data geometric?
  5. Name it by MGF. Write the MGF of the sum of five independent geometric (failures) waits with p = 0.7, and match it to the catalogue.
  6. The mixing story. Regenerate the data as Poisson counts with gamma-distributed rates (shape 5, scale 3/7) and confirm the mean, variance and P(0) match.
Done when: your fitted p̂ and r̂ are close to 0.7 and 5, you can quote how far the Poisson misses the tail, and you can explain in one sentence why the data are over-dispersed. Then compare with the check.
Check: the numbers (seed 0; exact values from the cards)
  • Step 1: mean 2.1443, variance 3.0821. The variance is above the mean, so the binomial and the Poisson are both out; the NB fits.
  • Step 2: p̂ = 0.6957 and r̂ = 4.903 (the truth: 0.7 and 5).
  • Step 3: the data give P(0) = 0.1690 and P(≥ 6) = 0.0481. The fitted Poisson gives 0.1171 and 0.0224: it underrates both the no-claim drivers and the heavy claimers, whose tail it roughly halves. The exact NB(5, 0.7) values are 0.1681 and 0.0473.
  • Step 4: exactly, P(X ≥ 1) = 0.8319 but P(X ≥ 2 | X ≥ 1) = 0.697, so it is not geometric (not memoryless).
  • Step 5: (0.7/(1 − 0.3eᵗ))⁵, which is the source's M(t) and NB(5, 0.7) in the failures convention.
  • Step 6: the negative-binomial card's gamma-Poisson simulation (10⁶ draws, seed 0) gives 2.1434, 3.0689 and 0.1681 against the exact 2.1429, 3.0612 and 0.1681.
