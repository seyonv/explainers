Checkpoint 3: totals, the normal and its limits

Ties together the normal and Chebyshev's bound, sums and the CLT, coupon collector, the normal approximation to the binomial, conditioning on a sum and how big a sample: when adding things up makes the normal the right tool, when an exact law or a bound is better, and how to run the question backwards.

How to use this page. Close the six cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before opening its Check. A wrong or shaky answer isn't a failure; it tells you exactly which section to reopen. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you have to work out which idea applies before you can answer.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which concept?You need the mean session length to within ±2 seconds at 95% confidence, and past data put the sd at 12 seconds. How many sessions do you sample, and which step do people most often get wrong?
    Check
    • n ≥ (zσ/E)² = (1.96 × 12 / 2)² = 138.3
    • round up: 139; rounding to the nearest can leave n below the bound
    • it assumes σ is known and the sample mean is near-normal; an estimated σ needs a t quantile and a little more

    Missed it? Reopen how big a sample, "The rule" and "Why it beats the alternatives".

  2. A.J. does 20 jobs, each with mean 50 and sd 10 minutes. A teammate says the total has sd 200 and so P(total < 900) ≈ 0.31. What went wrong, and what is the CLT's answer?
    Check
    • for independent jobs the variances add: 20 × 100 = 2000, sd 44.72, not 20 × 10
    • z = (900 − 1000)/44.72 = −2.236, Φ = 0.0127
    • adding sds assumes the jobs move in lockstep; it overstates the chance about 24×

    Missed it? Reopen sums and the CLT, "Adding the sds".

  3. which concept?A driver and a passenger are each hospitalized with probability 0.3; each hospital loss is uniform on [0, 1]. You are told only that the total loss was under 1. Which idea gives the expected number hospitalized, and why is the answer below 0.6?
    Check
    • condition on a sum: prior Bin(2, 0.3) × likelihood P(Sn < 1) = 1/n!, rescaled
    • joints 0.49, 0.42, 0.045; total 0.955; E[N | L < 1] = 0.51/0.955 = 102/191 ≈ 0.534
    • two losses together pass 1 half the time, so the condition mostly rules out the two-person case

    Missed it? Reopen conditioning on a sum, the table.

  4. For P(at least 50 of 100 customers wait over 10 minutes), with each waiting with chance e−1, where do you cut the normal and why? What does the exact sum say?
    Check
    • μ = 36.79, σ = 4.822; cut at 49.5, the left edge of bar 50
    • z = 2.636, normal tail 0.00419; exact 0.004708
    • cutting at 50 drops half of bar 50 and gives 0.00307, 35% low

    Missed it? Reopen the normal approximation, "Worked example: why the cut sits at 49.5".

  5. How many rolls, on average, to see all six faces of a die, and what is the variance? Which assumption lets you add the stage variances?
    Check
    • stages are geometric with p = 6/6, 5/6, …, 1/6: E[T] = 6H6 = 14.7
    • Var(T) = Σ (1 − p)/p² = 38.99
    • the stages are independent, because each stage's chance depends only on how many faces remain

    Missed it? Reopen coupon collector, the stage table.

  6. which concept?You know a latency distribution's mean and sd but nothing about its shape, and a customer asks what share of requests you can promise fall within 3 sd of the mean. What do you say, and why not 99.7%?
    Check
    • Chebyshev: at least 1 − 1/9 = 88.9%, for any shape with a finite variance
    • 99.73% is the normal's share, an estimate that holds only for bell-shaped data
    • a promise needs a guarantee; latency is usually right-skewed, so the normal isn't safe

    Missed it? Reopen the normal and Chebyshev, "The two rules" and "Is Chebyshev always the one to use?".

  7. A charity expects 2025 gifts with mean 3125 and sd 250. What total is exceeded only 10% of the time, and how sensitive is the answer to rounding z?
    Check
    • mean 2025 × 3125 = 6,328,125; sd 250 × √2025 = 11,250
    • 90th percentile: 6,328,125 + 1.2816 × 11,250 ≈ 6,342,542
    • with z = 1.28 it moves to 6,342,525: carry the exact z from inverting Φ

    Missed it? Reopen sums and the CLT, problem 4.

3 · Project: a tail audit

What it reinforces: exact laws vs the normal vs a bound, and where each wins (normal for a binomial, coupon collector, conditioning on a sum, Chebyshev), variances adding for totals (CLT) and running the normal backwards for n (sample size).

five questions, each answered three ways where possible: A P(Bin(100, e^-1) >= 50) pharmacy queue B P(T > 30) and P(T <= 6), T = rolls to see all 6 faces C P(U1 + U2 < 0.5), two uniform losses D P(|mean of 42 cars - mu| < 1), sigma = 3.3 E n to estimate p = e^-1 within +/- 0.05 at 95%

In about 50 lines of Python, standard library only (math.erf, math.comb, fractions):

  1. Exact. A: sum the binomial. B: the card's inclusion–exclusion cdf. C: the Irwin–Hall formula (or the triangle's area).
  2. Normal. A: np and np(1 − p), cut at 49.5. B: mean 14.7, sd √38.99, cut at 30.5 and at 6.5. C: mean 1, variance 2 × 1/12. D: 2Φ(√42/3.3) − 1.
  3. Bound. A and B: Cantelli, σ²/(σ² + t²) with t the distance to the first integer in the tail. D: Chebyshev, 1 − σ²/(nE²).
  4. Backwards. E: σ = √(p(1 − p)), then ⌈(zσ/E)²⌉, and the worst case with σ = 0.5.
  5. Verdict. For each row, write one sentence: is the normal high, low or fine, and which property of the distribution (skew, discreteness, few pieces) explains it?
Done when: your program prints one row per question with exact, normal and bound side by side, plus the two sample sizes, and every verdict names a cause. Then compare with the check.

  • A's bound is the card's Cantelli value; B's is 38.99/(38.99 + 16.3²) with 16.3 = 31 − 14.7. D's "exact" is the card's 10⁵-sample simulation for normal mileages.
  • E: σ = √(0.3679 × 0.6321) = 0.4823, n = ⌈357.3⌉ = 358; with p unknown, σ = 0.5 gives 385.
  • The pattern: the normal is excellent for means of many pieces (D) and in the middle, and it errs in both directions in tails, most where the total is skewed or built from few pieces (B, C). A bound never errs in the unsafe direction, but it is 25× loose in A.
