Checkpoint 2: two or more variables

Ties together joint and conditional tables, hierarchical models, order statistics and the range of a uniform sample, with the minimum rule from the exponential: slice a joint, build one from a hidden parent, or multiply when the variables are independent, and know which situation you are in.

How to use this page. Close the five cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before opening its Check. A wrong or shaky answer isn't a failure; it tells you exactly which section to reopen. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you have to work out which idea applies before you can answer.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. Five orders each take an independent Exp(0.1) time (mean 10 min). What is the expected time until all five are done, and why does the last one to finish account for 10 of those minutes?
    Check
    • race: while k clocks run, the next finish is Exp(kλ), mean 1/(kλ); memorylessness restarts the others at each finish
    • E[max] = 10(1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4 + 1/5) = 10 × 137/60 = 22.83 min
    • the last gap is one clock alone, rate λ, mean 1/λ = 10

    Missed it? Reopen order statistics, "The expected max: a race with shrinking fields".

  2. which concept?You have five readings from a sensor whose output is uniform on an unknown interval. The smallest is 2.1 and the largest is 2.7. What is your estimate of the interval's width, and which result tells you to adjust the raw range?
    Check
    • the sample range: E[R] = (b − a)(n − 1)/(n + 1), so on average the range is only 4/6 of the width for n = 5
    • raw range 0.6; unbiased width estimate 0.6 × 6/4 = 0.9
    • with so few points the estimate is still noisy; the correction only removes the bias

    Missed it? Reopen the range of a uniform sample, "Is this formula always the answer?".

  3. From the shoe-size table, someone reports E[S | H = 73] = 4.035. What did they compute, and what is the right answer?
    Check
    • 8.5 × 0.15 + 12 × 0.23 = 4.035 is E[S · 1{H = 73}]: the column still sums to only 0.38
    • divide by the column total: 4.035 / 0.38 = 807/76 ≈ 10.618

    Missed it? Reopen joint and conditional tables, step 5 of the trace.

  4. which concept?A dashboard shows readings R = S + noise, where the true signal S varies with variance 4 and the receiver adds noise of variance 1. An alert fires when a reading is more than 2 from the mean 10. The engineer expected it to fire about 5% of the time; it fires 37% of the time. Which idea explains the gap?
    Check
    • total variance: Var(R) = E[Var(R | S)] + Var(E[R | S]) = 1 + 4 = 5
    • the engineer used only the noise variance 1: P(|R − 10| > 2) = 0.046
    • with variance 5: 0.371; the spread between signals dominates

    Missed it? Reopen hierarchical models, "Why conditioning beats the alternatives".

  5. For five uniforms, Var(max) + Var(min) = 5/126, but Var(max − min) = 2/63. Why is the range's variance smaller than the sum, when for independent variables it would be equal?
    Check
    • Var(max − min) = Var(max) + Var(min) − 2 Cov(max, min)
    • the min and max of one sample are positively correlated: a sample that sits high pushes both up
    • so the covariance term is positive and the gap varies less; means still subtract by linearity (5/6 − 1/6 = 2/3)

    Missed it? Reopen the range of a uniform sample, "Why it beats the alternatives".

  6. which concept?You are given P(H = h) for each height and, for each height, the distribution of shoe size. You need the overall average shoe size, but nobody gave you the joint table. Which rule gets it directly, and what number do the source's columns give?
    Check
    • the tower rule: E[S] = Σ P(h) · E[S | H = h]
    • 0.30 × 9.083 + 0.32 × 9.8125 + 0.38 × 10.618 = 9.9
    • it's the hierarchical card's rule, read on the joint card's table: the parent is the height

    Missed it? Reopen joint and conditional tables, the tower check, and hierarchical models, "The two rules".

  7. Two counters, each Exp(mean 10), are both busy. What is P(the first frees up within 10 minutes), and which step of the calculation needs independence?
    Check
    • P(min > 10) = P(both > 10) = e−1 · e−1 = e−2 = 0.1353
    • so P(min ≤ 10) = 0.8647
    • independence is what lets "both > 10" split into a product

    Missed it? Reopen the exponential, "The minimum of exponentials is exponential".

3 · Project: a fan-out with a shared bad day

What it reinforces: the product rule for the max and the race for its mean (order statistics, exponential), a hidden parent with the tower rule and total variance (hierarchical models), slicing a two-way table (joint tables) and the spread between the fastest and slowest reply (sample range).

each day is fast or slow, with probability 1/2 each fast day: every replica's latency ~ Exp(rate 0.2 per ms) mean 5 ms slow day: every replica's latency ~ Exp(rate 0.1 per ms) mean 10 ms a request goes to 5 replicas and waits for all of them; given the day, the 5 latencies are independent

In about 40 lines of Python, standard library only:

  1. Given the day. For each day type compute P(max ≤ 10 ms) = F(10)⁵ and E[max] = H5/λ.
  2. Average over the day. Compute the unconditional P(max ≤ 10) and E[max] by the tower rule. Then compute the "naive" answer that averages F(10) over days first and raises it to the 5th power. Explain which is right and why.
  3. Total variance. Given the day, Var(max) = Σ 1/(jλ)² for j = 1..5. Combine the two days with E[Var] + Var(E) and say which term is larger.
  4. Slice the table. Build the 2 × 2 joint table of (day, max ≤ 10 or not). From it, compute P(slow day | a request took over 10 ms) and P(slow day | it finished within 10 ms).
  5. Spread. The gap between the fastest and slowest replica is the max of the other 4 after the first finishes. Give E[range] for each day type and overall.
  6. Check. Simulate 200,000 requests (draw the day, then 5 latencies) and compare P(max ≤ 10) and E[max].
Done when: your program prints both day types, the tower-rule answers next to the naive product, the variance split, the 2 × 2 table with both posteriors, E[range], and a simulation within about 0.003 of P(max ≤ 10). Then compare with the check.
Check: the numbers (computed with a Python version of this exact setup)
  • Given the day: slow P(max ≤ 10) = (1 − e−1)⁵ = 0.1009, E[max] = 22.83 ms; fast (1 − e−2)⁵ = 0.4833, E[max] = 11.42 ms.
  • Tower rule: P(max ≤ 10) = ½(0.1009 + 0.4833) = 0.2921; E[max] = ½(22.83 + 11.42) = 17.125 ms. The naive product ((0.6321 + 0.8647)/2)⁵ = 0.2348 is too low: on a fast day all five are fast together, and the shared day makes the replicas dependent even though they are independent given the day.
  • Variance: given the day 146.36 (slow) and 36.59 (fast), so E[Var] = 91.48; Var(E) = 32.59; total 124.06. Within-day noise is the larger part, but the day adds about a third on top.
  • Table: slow and ≤ 10: 0.0505; slow and > 10: 0.4495; fast and ≤ 10: 0.2417; fast and > 10: 0.2583. P(slow | > 10) = 0.4495/0.7078 = 0.6351; P(slow | ≤ 10) = 0.0505/0.2921 = 0.1727.
  • Range: after the first finish, the other 4 run a fresh race, so E[range] = H4/λ = 20.83 ms (slow), 10.42 ms (fast), 15.625 ms overall.
  • Simulation (seed 0, 200,000 requests): P(max ≤ 10) = 0.2921, E[max] = 17.11.
