Checkpoint 1: fitting and paying for a training run

Ties together a training step and its memory, compute budgets, ZeRO and FSDP, 4D parallelism, mixed precision and recompute and post-training: what a run costs, what it has to hold, how it is split across GPUs, and what it hands to the servers.

How to use this page. Close the six cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before opening its Check. A wrong or shaky answer isn't a failure; it tells you exactly which section to reopen. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you have to work out which idea applies before you can answer.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which concept?A team's 8B fine-tune on one big-memory GPU finishes step 1 and then runs out of memory on step 2, with the same batch. Which idea explains it, and what would you change first?
    Check
    • Adam's m and v (and the FP32 master copy) are allocated at the first optimizer step, so step 2 starts with 12 more bytes per parameter resident than step 1's forward did
    • on top of that, gradients persist while the next forward's activations climb
    • first change: shard the state (ZeRO/FSDP) or pick an optimizer with less state; activations are a separate lever

    Missed it? Reopen a training step, "The three phases of a step".

  2. Llama 3 405B needs 6 × 405e9 × 15.6e12 FLOPs. At the 400 TFLOP/s per GPU Llama 3 reported on 16,384 H100s, how many days of pure compute is that, and why is 400 TF the right number to divide by, not 1,979?
    Check
    • 3.79e25 ÷ (16,384 × 400e12) = 5.78e6 s ≈ 67 days
    • 400 TF is sustained throughput, 41% MFU of the 989 TF dense peak
    • 1,979 is the 2:4-sparse headline, which training doesn't use; dividing by it would halve the apparent utilization

    Missed it? Reopen compute budgets, "MFU vs HFU".

  3. which concept?Training the 8B at 32k tokens per sequence, you shard the state with ZeRO-3 over 8 GPUs and it still runs out of memory. A colleague proposes ZeRO over 64 GPUs. What does that fix, what doesn't it, and what do you propose instead?
    Check
    • ZeRO shards only model states: 128.5 GB ÷ 64 ≈ 2 GB instead of 16.1 GB per GPU
    • activations are per-GPU data and aren't sharded: 146 GB at 32k with attention recomputed, on every GPU
    • instead: full recompute (8.6 GB, about +33% FLOPs), or split the sequence (context parallelism) or hidden dimension (TP + SP)

    Missed it? Reopen ZeRO and FSDP, "Is FSDP always the answer?", and a training step, the activations table.

  4. A pipeline has 4 stages and 8 micro-batches. Give the bubble (idle over useful time) and the micro-batches of activations held at GPU 0 under AFAB, 1F1B and interleaved 1F1B with 2 chunks per GPU.
    Check
    • AFAB: 3/8 = 37.5%, holds all 8
    • 1F1B: 3/8 = 37.5%, holds p = 4: same time, less memory
    • interleaved, v = 2: 3/16 = 18.75%, holds 5.5 in the card's simulation, and sends twice as many point-to-point messages

    Missed it? Reopen 4D parallelism, "Pipeline schedules".

  5. which concept?On a T4 (no BF16), a model's training loss goes flat early. You find that a few percent of weight gradients are exactly zero, and that tiny weight updates don't change the weights. Name the two separate problems and the fix for each.
    Check
    • gradient underflow: values below FP16's smallest subnormal (2⁻²⁴) round to zero → loss scaling (dynamic: skip and halve on overflow)
    • lost updates: an update smaller than half the spacing at the weight rounds away → keep an FP32 master copy and update that
    • BF16 would remove the first problem, never the second

    Missed it? Reopen mixed precision, sections 1 and 2.

  6. ZeRO-2 on 8 GPUs for the 8B: state per GPU, and bytes sent per GPU per step compared with plain DP. Why the same?
    Check
    • 2Ψ + 14Ψ/8 = 16.06 + 14.05 = 30.1 GB (plain DP: 128.5 GB)
    • both send 2Ψ: 2 × 7/8 × 16.06 GB = 28.1 GB
    • an all-reduce is a reduce-scatter plus an all-gather; ZeRO-2 does the same two collectives and just keeps only its own gradient slice in between

    Missed it? Reopen ZeRO and FSDP, "What it costs in communication".

  7. In an R1-style GRPO run, a question gets 16 answers and all 16 are wrong. What does that question contribute to the update, and what did it cost?
    Check
    • mean = 0, std = 0, so every advantage is 0 (implementations guard the division)
    • no gradient from that question
    • but all 16 rollouts were generated, and at reasoning lengths generation is most of the RL bill

    Missed it? Reopen post-training, "RL, part 1" and "RL, part 2".

  8. which concept?A lab has a fixed training budget and expects to serve its model for two years at huge volume. The research team wants the Chinchilla-optimal size. Which idea argues against it, and in which direction should they move?
    Check
    • training costs 6N per token once; serving costs 2N per token for every token ever served
    • after about 3D served tokens, serving outspends training
    • move to a smaller N trained on more tokens: the 8B on 15T gives up 0.025 in the fit's loss and serves each token 4.4× cheaper than the fit's 35.7B optimum

    Missed it? Reopen compute budgets, "Why production models over-train".

3 · Project: plan a 70B training run, end to end

What it reinforces: the 6ND budget and MFU (compute budgets), the 16-byte state and the activation formula (a training step), FSDP sharding (ZeRO and FSDP), layout and the bubble (4D parallelism), the recompute choice (precision and recompute) and the hand-off to serving (post-training).

Llama-3.1-70B: N = 70.55e9, 80 layers, h = 8,192, a = 64, MLP width F = 28,672 data: 15T tokens · sequences of 8,192 · global batch 2,048 sequences (16.8M tokens) H100: 989 TF dense BF16, 80 GB, $3.99 per GPU-hour · assume 40% MFU

Write a short script (or a spreadsheet) and a one-page plan:

  1. Budget. FLOPs, H100-hours and dollars. How many days on 1,024 GPUs, and on 4,096?
  2. Memory. Training state at 16 bytes per parameter, and activations for one 8k sequence with attention recomputed (34·sbh·L).
  3. Layout. Use TP8 × PP4 × DP128 on 4,096 GPUs with FSDP over DP sharding grads and optimizer only. Per GPU: parameters (2Ψ ÷ 32), grads + optimizer (14Ψ ÷ (32 × 128)), and activations if each stage holds 20 layers, TP+SP divides them by 8, and 1F1B keeps p = 4 micro-batches of one sequence each. Does it fit?
  4. Bubble. Sequences per pipeline, the 1F1B bubble with v = 1 and with v = 2. Compare TP8 × PP8 × DP64.
  5. Limits. Is TP8 within the Scaling Book's TP bound F ÷ 2,200 inside a node?
  6. Hand-off. What does serving receive, and how many H100s does it need just for the BF16 weights?
Done when: your plan states the GPU count and days, shows that memory is not what binds at this scale, names the bubble you'd accept and the recompute mode you'd use, and says what ships to the servers. Then compare with the check.
Check: the numbers (our arithmetic with the cards' formulas)
  • Budget: 6 × 70.55e9 × 15e12 = 6.35e24 FLOPs → 4.46M H100-hours → $17.8M; 181 days on 1,024 GPUs, 45.4 days on 4,096.
  • Memory: state 1,128.8 GB; activations 34 × 67.1M × 80 = 182.5 GB per 8k sequence.
  • Layout: parameters 4.41 GB + grads/optimizer 0.24 GB = 4.65 GB; activations 20 layers × 34·sbh ÷ 8 = 5.70 GB per micro-batch, × 4 = 22.8 GB; total ≈ 27.5 GB of 80. Memory doesn't bind; FLOPs do, which is why you use 4,096 GPUs.
  • Bubble: 2,048 ÷ 128 = 16 sequences per pipeline → 3/16 = 18.75% at v = 1, 9.4% at v = 2. TP8 × PP8 × DP64: 32 sequences, 7/32 = 21.9%, so the shallower pipeline wins here.
  • Limits: 28,672 ÷ 2,200 ≈ 13-way, so TP8 (the node size) is within bounds.
  • Recompute: selective (attention only) is enough; full would add about a third to the 4.46M hours.
  • Hand-off: only the BF16 weights ship (141.1 GB): at least 2 H100s before any KV cache; the optimizer state stays behind.
