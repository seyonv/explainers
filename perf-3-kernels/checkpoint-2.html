Checkpoint 2: the matmul ladder

Ties together matmul I, matmul II, matmul III, matmul IV and occupancy and latency hiding: how each of siboehm's kernels cuts the bytes or loads per result, which bottleneck each step exposes next, and why the last 20% costs the most.

How to use this page. Close the five cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before opening its Check. A wrong or shaky answer isn't a failure; it tells you exactly which section to reopen. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you have to work out which idea applies before you can answer.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which concept?A colleague's shared-memory-tiled matmul (one result per thread, 1,024-thread blocks) shows most warps in "Stall MIO Throttle" at 66% occupancy. They propose shrinking the block to raise occupancy. Which idea tells you what's really wrong, and what would you change instead?
    Check
    • every FMA needs two shared-memory loads (one As, one Bs), so the MIO queue fills and the FMA units idle
    • occupancy isn't the limit: warps are often ready but "not selected"
    • give each thread several results so each loaded value feeds several FMAs: 2 → 1.125 → 0.25 loads per FMA (kernels 3 → 4 → 5)

    Missed it? Reopen matmul II, "What the profiler found", and matmul III, kernel 4.

  2. Give the two floors for siboehm's 4092² FP32 matmul on the A6000, and say how many times the minimum traffic the naive kernel moves.
    Check
    • 137 GFLOP ÷ 30 TF = 4.57 ms; 268 MB ÷ 768 GB/s = 0.35 ms: the problem is compute-bound up to ~13× the minimum traffic
    • naive: 8,185 floats per thread × 4092² threads × 4 B ≈ 548 GB = 2,046× the minimum
    • the problem is compute-bound; the naive kernel is not

    Missed it? Reopen matmul I, "Lower-bounding the fastest possible runtime".

  3. Kernels 1 and 2 compile to identical SASS, yet kernel 2 is 6.43× faster. What changed, and who does the work?
    Check
    • only the mapping from threadIdx.x to C: a warp now covers 32 columns of one row instead of 32 rows of one column
    • per step, a warp's reads go from 33 sectors (32 rows of A + one broadcast float of B) to 5 (one broadcast float of A + 32 neighbouring floats of B)
    • the hardware merges the accesses at run time; the compiler can't know the pointers are aligned

    Missed it? Reopen matmul II, kernel 2.

  4. which concept?Encouraged by kernel 5, you push the thread tile from 8 × 8 to 16 × 16. SMEM loads per FMA halve on paper, but the kernel gets slower. Which idea explains it?
    Check
    • registers: 256 accumulators plus 32 operand values exceed the 255-register limit per thread, so values spill to slow local memory
    • even before spilling, more registers per thread means fewer resident warps
    • the tile size is bounded by registers and occupancy, which is why siboehm autotunes it (an A100 preferred 4 × 4)

    Missed it? Reopen matmul III, "More is better", and occupancy, section 5.

  5. Write GMEM loads per result for a BM × BN block tile. Evaluate it for kernel 5's 128 × 128 at K = 4,092, give the GMEM-level FLOP/B, and say whether that clears a T4's FP32 ridge.
    Check
    • K·(BM + BN) ÷ (BM·BN) = K/64 ≈ 63.9 loads per result
    • 2K FLOPs ÷ (K/64 × 4 B) = 32 FLOP/B
    • the T4's ridge is ≈ 25, so the no-cache ceiling is min(8.1 TF, 32 × 320 GB/s) = 8.1 TF: compute-bound in principle

    Missed it? Reopen matmul III, "Loads per result".

  6. which concept?You ship the A6000's autotuned tile sizes to a fleet of A100s and they run 5% below a config tuned on the A100 itself. Which idea predicted this, and what does it imply for how you ship kernels?
    Check
    • autotuned configs are GPU-specific and unexplained: the A6000's winner is 0.95× of the A100's own best
    • the last-20% steps don't transfer between GPUs (the power law of effort)
    • tune per GPU (or per shape, as Triton's autotuner does), or rely on libraries that ship many kernels

    Missed it? Reopen matmul IV, "Kernel 9: autotuning".

  7. At 256², cuBLAS launches a GEMM plus a splitKreduce_kernel, and the reduce is 36% of the time. Why would a library choose that, and why does siboehm's kernel 10 fall behind at this size?
    Check
    • 256² cut into 64 × 32 tiles is only 32 output tiles: too few to keep every SM busy
    • split-K cuts the K loop across more blocks, each producing a partial sum, then reduces them
    • kernel 10's 128 × 128 block tile gives only 4 tiles at 256², so most SMs idle

    Missed it? Reopen matmul IV, "Across matrix sizes".

3 · Project: predict your T4 ladder before you run it

What it reinforces: the two floors (matmul I), loads per result at two levels (matmul II, matmul III), the resource limits behind tile choices (occupancy) and the warptile geometry (matmul IV).

  1. Floors. For 4096² FP32 on a T4 (8.1 TF, 320 GB/s), compute FLOPs, minimum bytes, both floors and their ratio.
  2. Calculator. Extend labs/matmul-loads.py (or write your own) to print, for kernels 1–5 and kernel 10: threads per block, shared memory per block, GMEM loads per result, SMEM loads per FMA, GMEM FLOP/B and the T4's no-cache ceiling min(8.1 TF, FLOP/B × 320 GB/s). Kernel 10 uses 128 threads, BM = BN = 128, BK = 16, TM = 8, TN = 4, WNITER = 4.
  3. Limits. Check every kernel against the T4's 1,024 threads per block and 64 KB of shared memory. When the notebook prints each kernel's registers, feed them into labs/occupancy.py.
  4. Predict. Write down each kernel's expected % of cuBLAS on the T4 by scaling the A6000 ladder, and mark the one step you expect to change most.
  5. Run. Open labs/matmul-siboehm-repo.ipynb on a T4 at 4096², and put measured next to predicted.
Done when: your calculator reproduces the cards' values (K/16 with 2 loads per FMA for kernel 3; K/32 and 1.125 for kernel 4; K/64 and 0.25 for kernel 5; ceilings 2.56, 5.12 and 8.10 TF on the T4), you have predicted vs measured for every kernel, and one sentence explaining the biggest mismatch with a number from your calculator.
Check: the numbers (computed with a Python version of the card formulas)
  • Floors: 2·4096³ + 4096² ≈ 137.5 GFLOP → 16.97 ms; 4 × 4096² × 4 B ≈ 268.4 MB → 0.84 ms; ratio ≈ 20.2×, so a kernel may move up to ~20× the minimum and stay compute-bound on the T4.
  • Kernels 1–2: 1,024 threads, no SMEM, 2K loads, 0.25 FLOP/B, 0.08 TF ceiling. Kernel 3: 1,024 threads, 8 KB, K/16, 2 per FMA, 8 FLOP/B, 2.56 TF. Kernel 4: 512 threads, 4 KB, K/32, 1.125, 16 FLOP/B, 5.12 TF. Kernel 5: 256 threads, 8 KB, K/64, 0.25, 32 FLOP/B, 8.10 TF (compute).
  • Kernel 10: 128 threads, (128·16 + 16·128) × 4 B = 16 KB, K/64 GMEM loads, 32 FLOP/B, 8.10 TF ceiling; 128 results per thread, and if each dotIdx step loads 8 values of A and 16 of B into registers, 24 loads for 128 FMAs = 0.1875 per FMA (our arithmetic from the card's settings).
  • All fit the T4's 1,024-thread and 64 KB limits. Kernel 3's occupancy on the T4 is 100% (the occupancy card).
  • A reasonable prediction: the structural steps (1 → 5) keep roughly their shape, while the late steps shift most, because the tile sizes were tuned for the A6000. The measured column is yours: the notebook hasn't been run by the author.
