Study path · Day 1.5 · one page

Just enough about the model

A short primer between Day 1 and Day 2 for readers new to machine learning: what a model is, what a token is, what one forward pass computes, and how a base model becomes an assistant. No training math, only what the inference cards build on. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

What today uses, and why

Day 2 opens up what the model computes on a GPU. If you've never seen what's inside a model, today gives you that picture with the gentlest cards in the hub, and nothing more. There's no training maths, and you skip every code block.

Zero to Hero cardsCards made from Karpathy's build-it-from-scratch video series (courses 0, 8, 9 and 10).
They explain each part of a GPT from zero, with pictures, which suits a first look. You read them only for the ideas.
Nucleus sampling cardOne card on how the next token gets chosen.
Sampling is the last box of every decode step on the Day 1 drawing. This is the plain-language version.
Models field guide: two stations and three principlesThe field guides' track on how models are trained.
You read only the intros and questions. You need the map of how a base model becomes an assistant, and where Day 1's thinking tokens come from, not the training details.
Your five sentencesA closed-book pretest you redo at the end.
Comparing the two versions shows you what you actually learned. This page keeps both in your browser.
The Day 1 drawing, bands 1 and 3The parts of the drawing about the model itself.
Today fills in the boxes you couldn't explain on Day 1. You finish by redrawing band 3 from memory.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Say what a language model is: a function that gives a probability to every possible next token. Generating text is calling it in a loop.
  • Explain what a token is, and why tokenization makes numbers, spelling and non-English text behave oddly.
  • Trace one forward pass (token ids → embeddings → attention and MLP blocks → logits → sampling) and say in one sentence what each part does.
  • Say what “the weights” are, and why a whole model is one file of learned numbers plus a fixed recipe.
  • Explain how pretraining makes a base model, and how fine-tuning and RL turn it into an assistant that follows instructions and thinks first.
  • Point at each of these on the Day 1 drawing, and say which ones set the speed: the weights and the KV cache.
How to use this page
  1. Scroll. The bar pinned at the top always says what the current step is and how to read it: Skim Read Pretest Checkpoint Optional Task.
  2. Each phase with a checkpoint opens with a Before you read box: prime, then try the checkpoint cold.
  3. Every card has two study boxes. The green Prime box at its top is read before the card. Study this card at its bottom is done after it, on Read steps only; Skim steps hide it.
  4. Tick Done as you finish a step. Take a break after each checkpoint.
  5. “Go deeper” sources stay closed. Open them only for a card whose questions you missed; Show here loads one inline.
