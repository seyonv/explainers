Time to first token (TTFT)

Also called first-token latency, or the "time to first chunk" when streaming. It's the wait before anything appears. Artificial Analysis reports it for every model and provider.

In one breath

TTFT is the time from sending a request to getting the first token back. It includes everything that happens before the model starts writing: the network, waiting in a queue, loading the model, reading your prompt, and any hidden thinking.

The parts of the wait, in plain words

Think of ordering at a restaurant. Network is the walk there and back. Queue is waiting for a table. Cold start is the kitchen still warming up. Prefill is the chef reading your order, and a long order takes longer to read. Only then does the first dish (token) come out.

How it differs from related ideas

✗ Output speed

How fast tokens come once they start. A provider can have low TTFT and slow output, or the reverse: Baseten has the lowest TTFT above (0.28 s) but is only 5th in output speed.

✗ End-to-end time

The whole response, TTFT plus writing time. It's what you feel with stream:false, because nothing shows until the end.

✗ Time to first answer token

For reasoning models: when the visible answer starts, after thinking ends. gpt-oss-120b (high): first token after 0.81 s, first answer token after 10.18 s.

✓ TTFT

The moment something appears. When you stream, it's the number that decides whether the app feels responsive.

When should I care about it?

Care about it for chat and autocomplete, and any time a person watches the screen. For long answers, background jobs, or requests with stream:false, it's the wrong thing to optimize: a 42-second answer on your M3 is 99% writing time, so TTFT barely matters. Also check whether a quoted TTFT includes thinking. For proprietary reasoning models, Artificial Analysis's TTFT includes the hidden thinking, which is why Sonnet 5 jumps from 1.39 s to 141 s.
