Tail latency (p50 vs p95 vs p99)

Also called percentiles, latency variance, jitter or the long tail. Shows up on every provider dashboard (OpenRouter tracks p50, p75, p90 and p99) and in every agent that makes more than one call.

In one breath. Send the same request many times and it won't take the same time twice. Sort the times: the middle one is the p50 (median), the one 95% of the way up is the p95, and 99% of the way up is the p99. The slow end of that list is the "tail". An agent makes many calls, so it lands in the tail far more often than a single call does.

Worked example 1: the same request, 20 times, on your Mac

same prompt, temperature 0, exactly 60 output tokens every time sorted times: 3.19 3.31 3.41 … 4.59 4.73 … 6.23 6.54 7.14 s p50 = middle of 20 = (10th + 11th) ÷ 2 = (4.59 + 4.73) ÷ 2 = 4.66 s p95 = 95% of the way up = 19th of 20 = 6.54 s (1.40× p50) max = slowest of 20 = 7.14 s (1.53× p50) writing speed ranged 8.9 → 19.9 tokens/s for identical work

Measured on your Apple M3 (Ollama 0.34.2, qwen3.5:4b Q4_K_M, think: false, num_predict: 60), 20 runs after one warm-up, 2026-09-21. The output was identical, yet the slowest run took 2.2× the fastest. The likely causes are other apps and heat on the laptop, but this run didn't isolate them. 20 runs is too few to estimate a p99.

P(at least one slow call) = 1 − P(no slow calls) = 1 − 0.99^N N = 10: 1 − 0.99^10 = 1 − 0.904 = 9.6% N = 100: 1 − 0.99^100 = 1 − 0.366 = 63.4% (Dean & Barroso's 100-server example: 63%) 10 calls in a row at Together AI's numbers: all typical: 10 × 0.61 s = 6.1 s one lands at p99: 9 × 0.61 s + 1 × 12.45 s = 17.9 s → 2.9× longer

The percentages assume each call is slow independently. The 10-call timing is illustrative: it uses the measured p50 and p99 above. The task waits for the slowest call on its critical path, the chain of steps that must happen in order. Calls run in parallel still wait for the slowest one.

Why it beats the alternatives

✗ Optimize the average

A good mean or median can sit on top of a 20× p99. A 100-call agent task hits that p99 at least once 63% of the time, and the average never shows it.

✗ Just raise the timeout

No more errors, but no faster either. A stuck call now blocks the task for the full 60 s or 10 min instead of failing and moving on.

✗ More retries, sooner

Retrying without a time budget restarts the wait, and piles more load onto an already slow server. That can make the tail worse for everyone.

✓ Measure and budget p95/p99, then hedge

Set targets on p95/p99 per call and per task. For slow calls, send a hedged (backup) request once the first has waited past its p95. In Dean & Barroso's test, a backup sent after 10 ms cut p99.9 from 1,800 ms to 74 ms for 2% more requests.

So is the median ever enough?

Yes, when nobody waits on a single call: overnight batch jobs, evals, bulk summarizing. There, total throughput and cost matter and the tail averages out. OpenRouter's advice is the same: judge batch jobs on p50, user-facing apps on p90/p99.

Hedging isn't free or always right. Hedging after the p95 wait adds at most 5% extra calls, but each one is paid for. It does nothing if the slowness is shared (a provider outage, or a huge prompt that is slow everywhere). It's also unsafe for calls with side effects, such as a tool that sends an email. In those cases, use a timeout plus a fallback provider instead, and cut the number of calls on the critical path.
