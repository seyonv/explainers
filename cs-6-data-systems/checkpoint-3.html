Checkpoint 3: sharing a GPU cluster fairly and visibly

Ties together fair-share GPU scheduling, backfilling and gang scheduling and the scheduler as a product: who gets the next GPUs, which job may use idle ones now, and how to make the answer believable to the people waiting.

How to use this page. Close the three cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before opening its Check. A wrong or shaky answer isn't a failure: it tells you exactly which section to reopen. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you have to work out which idea applies before you can answer.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which concept?A scheduler shares 16 GPUs and 128 cores between two teams and balances only GPU counts. Team B's tasks need 1 GPU and 16 cores each; after a while B holds every core and 2 GPUs sit idle. Which idea explains it, and what allocation would fix it?
    Check
    • counting only GPUs makes B's CPU-heavy tasks look cheap (1/16 of GPUs each), so B keeps getting tasks until the cores run out
    • DRF equalises dominant shares (each user's largest fraction of any resource)
    • on the card's cluster, DRF gives 5 tasks each, both at 5/8, with 15 of 16 GPUs busy

    Missed it? Reopen fair-share GPUs, "Worked example 2" and "The same cluster under four policies".

  2. Two users share the cluster equally. User A's jobs have priorities 3 and 1; user B's have 2, 1 and 1. What fraction of the cluster does each job get, and why does B1 (priority 2) get less than A1 (priority 3)?
    Check
    • share(job) = 50% × priority ÷ the user's priority sum
    • A1 37.5%, A2 12.5%, B1 25%, B2 12.5%, B3 12.5%
    • priorities only divide a user's own half; A has fewer, so each unit of A's priority is worth more

    Missed it? Reopen fair-share GPUs, "Worked example 1".

  3. which concept?Four distributed jobs of 2 learners each, on 4 machines with 2 GPUs per machine. Every job shows "Running 1/2", all 8 GPUs are held, and nothing has trained for hours. Which concept names this, and which policy prevents it?
    Check
    • a temporary deadlock from partial gangs: each job holds GPUs while waiting for the rest of itself (hold-and-wait)
    • gang scheduling: place a job's learners all at once or not at all
    • then 2 jobs train on all 8 GPUs and 2 wait holding nothing

    Missed it? Reopen backfilling and gangs, "Gang scheduling".

  4. With J0 holding 4 of 8 GPUs until t = 4, and J1 (8 GPUs × 2 h) at the head of the queue, why does EASY start J2 (2 × 3 h) at t = 0 but not J3 (2 × 6 h)?
    Check
    • J1's reservation (the shadow time) is t = 4, with extra = 8 − 8 = 0 spare GPUs then
    • J2 fits now and ends at 3 ≤ 4, so it can't delay J1
    • J3 would end at 6 > 4 and 2 > extra 0, so it would push J1 back

    Missed it? Reopen backfilling and gangs, the EASY hand trace.

  5. which concept?Finance reports the cluster at 95% utilization, researchers say it's always full, and training throughput is flat. Which idea reconciles all three, and what should the dashboard show instead?
    Check
    • utilization read as allocation counts GPUs handed out, not GPUs computing
    • the source's chart has 28% of allocated time at exactly 0%: 0.95 × 0.72 = 68.4% doing any work
    • show goodput (work that survives) and capacity waste; preempted-and-lost hours count as waste

    Missed it? Reopen the scheduler as a product, "Worked example 2" and the metrics table.

  6. User A submits 10 one-slot jobs, then user B submits 1, on one GPU. How long does B's job wait under FCFS and under per-user round-robin, and who pays for the difference?
    Check
    • FCFS: B1 waits 10 slots; round-robin: 1 slot
    • A's last job waits 10 instead of 9
    • fairness moves waiting from the light user to the heavy one; the total is unchanged

    Missed it? Reopen the scheduler as a product, the fair-queue code and "Is more explanation and fairness always better?".

  7. A preemption sends SIGKILL straight away and the job ends as FAILED_PREEMPTED after 3 hours of training. Which metric does that hurt that utilization never shows, and what one change would protect it?
    Check
    • goodput: 3 GPU-hours busy but lost, redone from scratch
    • utilization still counted those hours as busy
    • SIGTERM first, so the job can checkpoint before SIGKILL; preemption becomes a pause

    Missed it? Reopen fair-share GPUs, the Preemption row, and the scheduler as a product, the Reliability row.

3 · Project: a scheduler that explains itself

What it reinforces: DRF and its stopping point (fair-share GPUs), the shadow time and the EASY rule (backfilling and gangs), and pending reasons with numbers plus metrics that count waste (the scheduler as a product).

Start from drf in code/gpu-fair-share.py, simulate in code/backfill-gang-scheduling.py and why_pending in code/scheduler-as-product.py.

  1. Why is my task pending (two resources)? Run DRF on the card's cluster (16 GPUs, 128 cores; A = 2 GPUs + 8 cores, B = 1 GPU + 16 cores). When it stops, print a reason for each team's next task that names the resource that's short and by how much.
  2. Why is my job pending (backfill)? Make the EASY loop print one line for every queued job it considers at every decision time: started, or the exact reason it waits (doesn't fit, or would end after the shadow time and doesn't fit in extra).
  3. Metrics. For FCFS and EASY, print each job's wait, the average wait and the idle GPU-hours before t = 4.
  4. Change one estimate. Set J2 to 5 hours and rerun step 3 for EASY. Which job backfills now?
  5. Fair queue. Using fair, print B1's wait when A submits 10 one-slot jobs first, under FCFS and under round-robin.
Done when: every waiting task or job has a one-line reason with numbers in it, and your metrics match the check. Then explain in one sentence why the reasons in steps 1 and 2 would stop a user from cancelling and resubmitting.
Check: the numbers (computed with the cards' own code)
  • DRF stops at 5 + 5 tasks: 15 GPUs and 120 cores used, so 1 GPU and 8 cores free. A: "needs 2 GPUs, 1 free, waiting for 1 more". B: "needs 16 cores, 8 free, waiting for 8 more".
  • EASY at t = 0: J1 waits (8 > 4 free; reserved at t = 4, extra 0); J2 starts (0 + 3 ≤ 4); J3 waits (0 + 6 > 4 and 2 > 0); J4 waits (needs 4, 2 free). At t = 3: J4 waits (3 + 2 > 4), J3 waits (3 + 6 > 4). J1 starts at t = 4.
  • FCFS waits [4, 6, 6, 6], average 5.5 h, 16 idle GPU-hours before t = 4. EASY waits [4, 0, 6, 6], average 4.0 h, 10 idle GPU-hours.
  • J2 at 5 h: J2 no longer fits the window, but J4 (4 GPUs, ends at 2) does, so J4 starts at 0: waits J1 4, J2 6, J3 6, J4 0, average 4.0 h, 8 idle GPU-hours.
  • Fair queue: B1 waits 10 slots under FCFS and 1 under round-robin; A's last job waits 10 instead of 9.
