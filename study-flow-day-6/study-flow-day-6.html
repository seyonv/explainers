Study path · Day 6 · one page

Memory is the product: attention and the KV cache

Day 6 of the inference study plan: how attention is computed without storing every score, how to make each token's KV smaller, how engines manage KV memory in pages, and how they reuse it across requests. It covers the Attention and the KV cache course and Serving station 4. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

What today uses, and why

Day 3 showed that KV memory, not maths, decides how many users fit. Day 6 is about that memory: computing attention without wasting it, making each token's share smaller, packing it in pages and reusing it across requests.

Attention and the KV cache courseCourse 4 of the Fast LLM inference series: FlashAttention, GQA and MLA, PagedAttention, RadixAttention, Mooncake.
Each paper is walked section by section. The FlashAttention internals are optional; the memory-management half is the core.
Serving station 4: memory is the productA field-guide lesson with read-with questions and a closed-book check.
It checks the day against real engines' paged and cached memory.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Explain how FlashAttention computes exact attention without storing the full score matrix, and why that saves memory traffic rather than maths.
  • Compare grouped-query attention, multi-head latent attention and a quantized KV cache by bytes per token and what each costs.
  • Explain PagedAttention: KV memory as pages, sharing with copy-on-write, and why it lets a bigger batch fit.
  • Explain how RadixAttention and Mooncake reuse KV across requests, and when reuse pays for itself.
  • Say what changes for long contexts: sliding windows, ring attention and sparse attention.

Phase 2 · Smaller KV, fuller pool

~1.75 h · 0 / 7

Three ways to shrink each token's KV, then paging, which stops memory being wasted between requests.

Before you read · 2 steps

1 · Prime · 2 min. Read this list of what each card will let you do. Don't open the cards yet.

  • Grouped-query attention. Compute KV bytes, sequences that fit and decode throughput for any G, and explain why the paper picks 8 groups over MQA.
  • Multi-head latent attention (DeepSeek). Compute MLA's KV per token, explain how absorption means per-head K and V are never built, and say why RoPE needs its own small key.
  • Quantizing the KV cache. Compute capacity and throughput for FP8, INT4 and KIVI-2, and explain why keys go per-channel and values per-token.
  • PagedAttention I: KV memory as pages. Name the three kinds of waste, trace a block table through a few decode steps, and compute how many requests fit with reserve-max versus paged allocation.
  • PagedAttention II: sharing, copy-on-write and preemption. Count blocks with and without sharing for samples and beams, trace copy-on-write, and choose between swap and recompute when memory runs out.
  • PagedAttention III: implementation and results. Explain the source of vLLM's speedup, read a normalized-latency curve, and predict how many KV blocks vLLM allocates on an H100.
