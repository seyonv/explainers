Study path · Day 9 · one page

Operate and price a fleet

Day 9 of the inference study plan, the last: many replicas behind one API, what a token really costs, and how to run the whole thing safely. It covers Part 5 of the request-scheduler course, the cost-of-a-token half of the Training and cost per token course and Serving station 7, and ends with the Serving guide's last project and capstone. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

What today uses, and why

The last day turns an engine into a service: many replicas behind one API, what a token really costs, and how to change it safely. It ends with the Serving guide's last project and its capstone.

Request scheduler course, Part 5Routing and autoscaling, the whole system drawn, real companies, and a final explain-it-back.
It ties the path together at the level of a whole system.
Training and cost per token course, the cost halfCourse 7 of the Fast LLM inference series.
Cost per token, choosing hardware, cold starts, routing on cached KV and safe releases. The training half is optional.
Serving station 7, project 5 and the capstoneThe last lesson and the last two projects.
Pricing and operating a real deployment, measured end to end.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Route requests across many replicas, and decide when to add or remove one.
  • Explain cold starts and scale to zero, and when routing a request to the replica that already holds its KV pays.
  • Draw the whole serving system from the API gateway to the GPU, and say what each layer is for.
  • Work out the cost of a million tokens at a latency target, and how the batch size moves it.
  • Choose hardware for a workload from its compute, memory bandwidth, memory size and price.
  • Plan a safe release: canaries, failures and quality checks.

Phase 2 · What a token costs

~2.75 h · 0 / 8

The cost-of-a-token half of the Training and cost per token course, including routing on cached KV and cold starts, which build on the first card.

Before you read · 2 steps

1 · Prime · 2 min. Read this list of what each card will let you do. Don't open the cards yet.

  • $ per million tokens and the batch dial. Compute $/M output and input for any batch, context, price and utilization, and pick the operating point that a per-user speed promise allows.
  • KV-aware routing: sending requests where their cache lives. Compute a KV-aware router's choice by hand, predict when stickiness beats it and when it melts, and price what routing saves.
  • Autoscaling, cold starts and scale to zero. Price a traffic day under different autoscaling policies, break a cold start into its parts and compute the one you can compute exactly, and decide how many warm replicas to keep.
  • Choosing hardware: FLOPS, bandwidth, memory and price. Name the binding resource for a workload, rank GPUs by $/hr divided by it, and spot the traps in a spec sheet or a vendor comparison.
  • Reliability and quality: failures, canaries and evals. Turn a failure rate into expected failures, a checkpoint interval and an availability figure, and lay out a rollout that catches a quality regression before users do.
