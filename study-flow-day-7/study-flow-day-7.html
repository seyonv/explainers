Study path · Day 7 · one page

Cheaper decode: inside engines, and structured output

Day 7 of the inference study plan: what a real engine is made of, the two ways to make each decode step cheaper (fewer bytes, more tokens per step), and how an engine forces valid JSON. It covers the Inside inference engines course, the core of the Structured outputs course and Serving station 5, and ends with the Serving guide's prediction-ledger project. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

What today uses, and why

Decode is limited by memory reads. Day 7 covers the two ways to make each step cheaper, fewer bytes and more tokens per step, plus the engine features that cost time per token, and ends with the project that measures all of them.

Inside inference engines courseCourse 5 of the Fast LLM inference series.
What vLLM and SGLang are made of, quantization for serving and speculative decoding. The Orca cards repeat Day 2, so they are optional.
Structured outputs course, eight cardsThe core of a course on forcing valid JSON.
Only the cards the ledger project needs: the mechanism, its cost in time and its effect on quality.
Serving station 5 and project 3A lesson, then the prediction ledger on the Day 4 GPU.
The station checks the reading; the project measures four mechanisms after predicting each one.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Name the parts of a real engine (vLLM, SGLang) and what each does on every step.
  • Explain how quantization for serving cuts the bytes read per step, and how to check what it costs in quality.
  • Explain speculative decoding: why its output is exact, when it helps, and why it fades at large batch sizes.
  • Explain how an engine forces valid JSON by masking tokens, what that costs in time, and why valid is not the same as correct.
  • Predict, then measure, four mechanisms on the lab GPU, keeping a ledger of every prediction.

Phase 1 · Inside an engine

What vLLM and SGLang are made of. The two Orca cards repeat Day 2's continuous batching, so they are optional; the two Sarathi cards are required because later cards build on them.

Before you read · 2 steps

1 · Prime · 2 min. Read this list of what each card will let you do. Don't open the cards yet.

  • Sarathi-Serve I: why prefill stalls decode. Compute how much a prompt of P tokens stretches a decode step, explain from the roofline why the first few hundred prompt tokens are nearly free, and place each scheduler on the throughput–latency trade-off.
  • Sarathi-Serve II: chunked prefill and stall-free batching. Explain why chunking and stall-free ordering are both needed, choose a token budget from a TBT target, and say what each choice costs in TTFT.
  • The scheduler: KV budget, admission and preemption. Compute the KV pool for a GPU and model, predict roughly when a burst will start preempting, and choose the knobs for a latency or throughput target.
  • Anatomy of vLLM and SGLang. Follow one request through vLLM V1's processes, compute what a given CPU cost per step does to throughput at small and large batch, and explain which fix hides which part of the overhead.
  • Serving many LoRA adapters. Compute an adapter's size and per-step cost on the running example, and explain why unmerged adapters plus a gather kernel keep batching intact.
