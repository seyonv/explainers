Reading plan: what to read, how much, and when

Also called the 80/20 cut of the wafer-ai GPU perf engineering resource list, or the DEEP / SKIM / SKIP triage. Every card in this series ends with a “Read next” line that points at one row of these tables.

In one breath

The resource list holds 120 distinct links, almost all of them primary sources. About 27 deserve a deep read and about 44 a targeted skim. The other 49 are reference material that you'll only open when a job needs them. Read them in course order, next to the cards: first the course's cards, then the rows in its table.

The three verdicts

Think of a new job's wiki: you read the onboarding pages cover to cover, you skim the design docs for the one section your team owns, and you only search the API reference when something breaks.

The 49 SKIPs, in one paragraph

Reference manuals (4). You look things up in them; you don't read them through: CUDA Programming Guide (full), NVCC, PTX ISA, CUDA Binary Utilities.

Advanced kernel stacks (13). These matter when your job is writing production Hopper or Blackwell kernels, not while you learn the mechanism: decoupled look-back scan, CUTLASS 3.x design, CuTe layout algebra, CuTe GEMM tutorial, CUTLASS pipelines, CUDA Tile IR, CUDA Tile repo, Colfax TMA tutorial, tcgen05, Transformer Engine, Blackwell Tuning Guide, CUTLASS Blackwell docs, Triton paper.

Non-NVIDIA hardware (16). The series is NVIDIA-first, so promote these if your company runs AMD, TPU or Trainium: Composable Kernel, AITER, HipKittens, ROCm Compute Profiler, CDNA 4 whitepaper, CDNA 4 ISA, MI350 counters, Pallas design, Pallas TPU model, TPU v1, TPU v4, Ironwood, NKI programming model, Trainium/Inferentia2 arch, Trainium3 arch, NKI performance guide.

Engine side-topics (6). These are real topics, but not on the path to cheap, fast text serving: CacheGen, Guiding LLMs the Right Way, Fairness in serving, MInference, vLLM multimodal inputs, TensorRT-LLM repo.

Interconnect and transport specs (4). The collective and bandwidth numbers are what matter, and the specs are for people building fabrics: multi-node NVLink tuning, UALink 1.0, Ultra Ethernet 1.0.3, NIXL.

Production and frontier niche (6). These are older, narrower or still moving: Clockwork, ServeGen, MLPerf Endpoints, KernelBench-Verified, SOL-ExecBench, Rubin watchlist.

How it differs from related ideas

✗ Reading the list top to bottom

The README says to read “Start here” first, then: “After that, use it as a reference.” It's a reference, not a syllabus. Straight through, you'd spend weeks in the 49 SKIP items, such as ISA manuals and other vendors' chips.

✗ Reading only blogs and tutorials

The list is primary sources by design. Its CONTRIBUTING file says, “Do not submit summaries, generic tutorials, marketing pages, broad surveys”. Blogs such as siboehm's count only as “a direct implementer report with code, measurements, and enough detail to reproduce the result.”

✗ Doing only the cards

The cards are the map and the papers are the territory. A card compresses a paper to one mechanism and one worked example. The details you're asked about in a design review, such as edge cases and baselines, live in the source.

✓ Cards first, then the named sections

Each card gives you the shape, so its paper reads fast. Then read only the sections named in its DEEP or SKIM row. That's about 93 hours, not the whole list.

Is this the right plan for everyone?

No, it's a starting point tuned for one goal: following the whole pipeline at an NVIDIA-based company that serves LLMs fast and cheaply. Change it when your job changes.

  • AMD, TPU or Trainium shop: promote that vendor's SKIP group (Composable Kernel and AITER, Pallas and the TPU docs, or NKI and the Trainium architecture pages) to SKIM or DEEP, and drop the Hopper tuning guide to SKIP.
  • Hired to write production kernels: promote CUTLASS 3.x, CuTe, the TMA tutorial and tcgen05 from SKIP to DEEP. Those rows are skipped here only because the mechanism comes first.
  • Hired onto routing or platform work: perf-3's DEEP kernel rows can drop to SKIM (our judgement), and the perf-7 production rows move up.
