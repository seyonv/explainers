Backprop through attention, three tokens

Attention backward, softmax backward, dQ,dK,dVdQ, dK, dVdQ,dK,dV, gradients of Wq,Wk,Wv\Wq, \Wk, \WvWq​,Wk​,Wv​ · no Raschka section (the book leaves this step to autograd)

Prime · 2 minutes before you read

The problem: The loss has a gradient for attention's output, but Wq\WqWq​, Wk\WkWk​ and Wv\WvWv​ sit behind two matrix products and a softmax. Without walking the gradient back through them, those weights can't learn, and you can't tell why some of them get tiny gradients.

Where it fits: Builds on the linear rule (dW=x⊤dydW = x^\top dydW=x⊤dy, dx=dy W⊤dx = dy\,W^\topdx=dyW⊤) from grad-linear-layer and on the forward attention cards (scores, softmax, AVAVAV). It takes do=dx1do = dx_1do=dx1​ from the residual and hands dadada to LayerNorm 1's backward pass.

The loss now has a gradient for ooo, attention's output. To train Wq\WqWq​, Wk\WkWk​ and Wv\WvWv​, that gradient has to get back through a matrix product, a softmax and another matrix product. It is the linear rule at every matrix product, with one softmax step in the middle, and every number fits on this page.

C=AVC = AVC=AV hands the blame out two ways

The first step is the plain linear rule from the grad-linear-layer card: o=CWoo = C\Woo=CWo​, so dC=do Wo⊤dC = do\,\Wo^\topdC=doWo⊤​. Row 2 comes out [−0.2716, 0.6220, −0.6947, −0.4978][-0.2716,\ 0.6220,\allowbreak\ -0.6947,\ -0.4978][−0.2716, 0.6220, −0.6947, −0.4978].

Softmax backward keeps only the differences

The scores SSS make the weights through softmax. Raise one score and its share goes up, but every other share in the row goes down to keep the sum at 1. So what matters for a score is not its dAdAdA alone, but how its dAdAdA compares with the row's average.

That's the formula. For row iii: work out the average dAdAdA, weighted by the shares, ∑kdAikAik\sum_k dA_{ik} A_{ik}∑k​dAik​Aik​. Subtract it from each dAdAdA. Then multiply by the share AAA, because a token with almost no share barely moves when its score moves.

Why Wq\WqWq​ barely moves on this sentence

The largest gradient on each projection, from tiny.out:

weightslargest |gradient|path back from dCdCdC
Wv\WvWv​0.6413one matrix product (A⊤A^\topA⊤), no softmax
Wk\WkWk​0.2937through softmax and ÷2\div 2÷2; gets every token's key

Follow the size of the gradient down the QQQ path. The largest entry of dAdAdA is 0.9005. After softmax backward the largest entry of dSdSdS is 0.1109, and after ÷2\div 2÷2 it's 0.0555. Three things shrink it:

  • Row 0 is fixed. One allowed key, so the share is 1 whatever the query. dS[0]=0dS[0] = 0dS[0]=0, so dQ[0]=0dQ[0] = 0dQ[0]=0.
  • Row 1 is near 50/50, and both options look about the same to the loss. Its two dAdAdA values are 0.0549 and 0.0759. Moving weight from one to the other barely matters, so dS[1]dS[1]dS[1] is only ±0.0053\pm 0.0053±0.0053.
  • Only row 2 is left, and the d\sqrt{d}d​ scale halves its dSdSdS.

QQQ and KKK only change the loss by changing the shares, and a share that can't move, or whose options are nearly equal, sends back almost nothing. Wv\WvWv​ skips that step: values change the output directly. To check the hand chain, nudge Wq[0,3]\Wq[0,3]Wq​[0,3] by ±0.001\pm 0.001±0.001 and rerun the forward pass. The loss changes by −0.2320 per unit of nudge, the same as the gradient.

Principle

Attention backward is the linear rule, plus one softmax step that only passes differences

Every matrix product sends the gradient back with a transpose. Softmax is the one step that isn't a matrix product: it subtracts the row's average, so the gradient on a score says "more than the others" or "less than the others", never just "more". Wherever a tensor was used more than once, its gradients add.

Buys you: the ability to say, before running anything, which weights will get a small gradient and why: a fixed row or a near-tie sends almost nothing back to the queries and keys.

Where this simplification breaks
  • The tiny model has one head. With many heads, each head runs these lines on its own slice of columns, and the slices are glued back together before Wo\WoWo​.
  • The small Wq\WqWq​ gradient is about this sentence and these random weights. With 3 tokens, row 0 always gets nothing; a longer sentence has many more rows that can move.
  • Real code doesn't store AAA. Fast kernels such as FlashAttention recompute it in blocks during the backward pass to save memory. The maths is the same.
  • Dropout on the attention weights (Raschka §3.5.2) is left out here. With it, the backward pass also multiplies by the same dropout mask.
  • In practice you never write these lines: PyTorch's autograd does it, which is why the book doesn't. The value of doing it once is knowing what autograd is computing.

Check yourself

1. Every row of dSdSdS sums to 0. Why must that be true for any softmax?

Show the answer

Adding the same number to every score in a row doesn't change the softmax, so it doesn't change the loss. The slope in that direction is 0, and that slope is the row's sum. Row 2: −0.1103+0.1109−0.0006=0-0.1103 + 0.1109 - 0.0006 = 0−0.1103+0.1109−0.0006=0.

2. Why is dV[2]dV[2]dV[2] so much smaller than dV[1]dV[1]dV[1]?

Show the answer

dV=A⊤dCdV = A^\top dCdV=A⊤dC, so a value row is blamed only by the tokens that listened to it, scaled by how much. Only sat listened to its own value, at 0.0131. cat's value was heard at 0.5021 and 0.8132.

3. Suppose cat's row of AAA were [0.9, 0.1][0.9,\ 0.1][0.9, 0.1] instead of near 50/50, with the same dAdAdA values (0.0549, 0.0759). Would dS[1]dS[1]dS[1] get bigger or smaller?

Show the answer

Smaller. With two keys, dS10=A10 A11 (dA10−dA11)dS_{10} = A_{10}\, A_{11}\, (dA_{10} - dA_{11})dS10​=A10​A11​(dA10​−dA11​). 0.9⋅0.1=0.090.9 \cdot 0.1 = 0.090.9⋅0.1=0.09, less than 0.4979⋅0.5021≈0.250.4979 \cdot 0.5021 \approx 0.250.4979⋅0.5021≈0.25. A lopsided row is harder to move. Near 50/50 the slope is as big as it gets; row 1's gradient is small because its two dAdAdA values are close.
