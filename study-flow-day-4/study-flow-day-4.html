Study path · Day 4 · one page

Measure a real server

Day 4 of the inference study plan: how to load-test a server, why its latency curve has a knee, what to watch while it runs, and where to run it. It ends with the Serving guide's load-curve lab: an open model behind an API on a rented GPU, measured from 1 to 128 users. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

What today uses, and why

Days 1 to 3 explained why a server behaves as it does and let you predict it on paper. Day 4 is where you measure one. The reading is short and covers only what the lab needs that earlier days didn't: how to load-test, why the curve bends, and what to watch while it runs.

Serving project 2: serving lab v1A project brief from the Serving guide: an 8B model behind an API on a rented GPU, measured from 1 to 128 users.
The point of the day. You read its brief first, so every card answers a line of it, and you do it last.
Benchmarking a server, and Measuring a serverTwo cards you've passed or skimmed before.
They hold the load-testing method: what to send, warm-up and repeats, and the difference between a test that waits for replies and one that doesn't. The lab's numbers mean nothing without them.
Queueing and the kneeA new card in the request-scheduler course.
The lab asks you to mark the knee of the curve and say what breaks first. This card is the explanation: waiting grows much faster than load.
Reading the server's countersA new card in the engines course.
The lab asks you to plot the engine's own counters. This card says which ones matter and what each reading means, so you can tell a client problem from a memory limit from a queue.
Modal's two pagesA benchmarking method and a guide to fast inference, from a company that runs GPUs on demand.
A second opinion from outside the field guide. They sweep the request rate where the lab sweeps users, and you should be able to say why.
Serving failures F03, F05, F14 and F21Four short bug reports from the Serving guide.
Each is a way the lab goes wrong. Reading them first means you recognise the symptom when it happens to you.
Serving warm-upsFive small projects leading up to the lab.
Rungs A to C are the cheap practice runs from Days 1 to 3. Today uses the page for where to run the lab and for the two checks to pass before the full sweep.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Design a load test: a fixed, realistic workload, a warm-up, repeats, and the difference between holding the number of requests in flight fixed and holding the request rate fixed.
  • Explain why the wait grows much faster than the load near saturation, and use Little's law to turn requests in flight into a request rate.
  • Predict where the curve bends and which limit breaks first, before running anything.
  • Read the engine's own counters during a run, and tell a client that isn't sending load from a server that is out of memory or queueing.
  • Say why the lab runs on a dedicated GPU pod and not a serverless platform, and what each costs.
  • Serve an 8B model with vLLM behind an API, produce its latency-throughput curve with repeats, mark the knee and name the metric that breaks first.

  4. Tick Done as you finish a step. Take a break after each checkpoint.
