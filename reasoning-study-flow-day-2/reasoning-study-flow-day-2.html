Reasoning study path · Day 2 · one page

From text predictor to assistant

Day 2 of the reasoning-models path: how a model that only continues text becomes one that answers you. Reinforcement learning from zero, reward models trained on people's preferences, and DPO, which gets the same result without the RL loop. It follows station 6 of the Models field guide. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

What today uses, and why

Every reasoning method on Days 3 and 4 is either a way to pick better answers or a way to train on scores instead of copies. Day 2 sets up the second idea with the recipe that made chat assistants.

Three cards written for this pathPolicy gradient, RLHF, and DPO.
Each one works a single example by hand, so the papers after it land.
Models field guide, station 6InstructGPT, DPO and LoRA, with the guide's questions and checks.
The original papers, read for their key figures rather than end to end.
Project 4Base model to assistant, from the Models guide.
Recommended. Seeing SFT change a base model's answers makes the day concrete.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Explain how reinforcement learning differs from training on correct answers, and what a baseline is for.
  • Draw InstructGPT's three steps and say what each one trains on.
  • Say why a reward model learns from pairs of answers, and what a KL penalty stops the model from doing.
  • Explain what DPO removes from RLHF, and what it gives up by training offline.

Phase 1 · Reinforcement learning from zero

~1.25 h · 0 / 2

Supervised training copies a right answer. Reinforcement learning has no right answer to copy, only a score for what the model tried. This phase is the one idea every later method builds on.

Before you read · 2 steps

1 · Prime · 2 min. Read these questions. Keep them in mind for every step below.

  • In the policy gradient, why do the environment's dynamics drop out, and what does reward-to-go remove?
  • Why does subtracting a baseline leave the gradient unbiased, and why is GRPO's group mean such a baseline?

Phase 2 · Learning what people prefer

~2 h · 0 / 6

“Be helpful” can't be checked by a script, but people can say which of two answers is better. RLHF trains a model to predict those choices, then optimises against it.

Before you read · 2 steps

1 · Prime · 2 min. Read these questions. Keep them in mind for every step below.

  • In InstructGPT's three-step figure, what does each stage train on, and which stage does DPO replace?
  • Why does a Bradley-Terry reward model learn only reward differences, and what does that do to its scale?
  • In DPO's derivation, where did the reward model go, and what job does the frozen reference model do?

Checkpoint08 / 9

Checkpoint: preferences

Answer every question before opening its answer. Note which card each miss points to. From the Models guide's closed-book check for station 6. Take a break after.

  1. Your reward model is 66% accurate on held-out pairs. Which numbers do you need before calling that a success?

    Show the answer
    • The 'longer answer wins' baseline on the same pairs
    • Accuracy on the length-matched subset (lengths within 10%)
    • Chance with its interval: 50% ± 3.1 points at 95% on 1,000 pairs
    • If length alone reaches most of the 66%, the model learned length (F14)

    Missed it? Models failure F14 · Reward Modeling (TRL RewardTrainer documentation)

  2. DPO skips the reward model and the sampling loop. What does that save, and what does training offline cost?

    Show the answer
    • It saves training a reward model and generating rollouts: two forward passes (policy and frozen reference) per response in each pair
    • It learns only from the fixed pairs, never from the model's own new answers
    • The margin can grow by pushing rejected answers down while chosen ones also fall (F13)
    • That is why online RL came back for tasks where answers can be checked

    Missed it? Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model · Models failure F13
