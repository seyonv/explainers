Compute-bound or memory-bound? The roofline intuition

Also called the roofline model, arithmetic intensity, ops:byte, or the ridge point. Anyone who says "decode is memory-bound" or "prefill is compute-bound" is using this idea. Here it's applied to Llama-3.1-8B on one H100.

In one breath

Every operation needs some math (FLOPs) and has to move some bytes between memory and the chip. It takes about as long as the slower of the two: FLOPs ÷ peak FLOP/s, or bytes ÷ memory bandwidth. Arithmetic intensity (FLOPs per byte) tells you which one wins, and the hardware's ridge point is the dividing line: on an H100 it's 989e12 ÷ 3.35e12 ≈ 295 FLOPs per byte.

Kitchen version: a chef who can make 295 cuts in the time a runner brings one ingredient from the pantry sits idle on a recipe that needs one cut per ingredient, and keeps the runner waiting on one that needs 500.

The formula

T≈max( FLOPsCmath time , bytesWmemory time )
I=FLOPsbytes I*=CW I>I*⇔compute-bound
Cpeak compute, FLOP/sH100 BF16 dense: 989e12 Wmemory (HBM) bandwidth, bytes/sH100: 3.35e12 FLOPsmultiplies + adds the operation needsmatmul [B,D]·[D,F]: 2BDF bytesdata read from and written to HBMBF16: 2 bytes per number Iarithmetic intensity of the operationproperty of the work I*ridge point (the chip's ops:byte ratio)property of the hardware: 295
T ≈ max(FLOPs / C, bytes / W) I = FLOPs / bytes I* = C / W = 989e12 / 3.35e12 = 295.2

Why comparing I with I* works: FLOPs/C > bytes/W is the same inequality as FLOPs/bytes > C/W. Taking the max assumes the chip overlaps math with memory traffic perfectly. With no overlap you'd take the sum, which is at most 2× the max (Scaling Book Part 1).

Batching moves you along the roof

For a matmul with B tokens, I = BDF ÷ (BD + DF + BF), which is about B when B is much smaller than D and F. The weights are read once however many tokens share them, so batching tokens raises intensity almost for free. Why it beats the alternatives

✗ Count FLOPs only

16.06 GFLOP ÷ 989 TF/s says a decode step takes 0.016 ms. The real floor is 4.79 ms, so the estimate is about 300× too fast, and it would tell you to buy more compute you can't use.

✗ Count bytes only

Right for decode, wrong for prefill: the 512-token matmul would be predicted at 40.7 µs, but the math takes 60.8 µs. Bytes-only also says batching is free forever, which stops being true at the ridge.

✗ Just benchmark it

You get a number, but not why. A 35 µs kernel could be excellent (it's at the bandwidth roof) or terrible, and the benchmark alone doesn't say whether to fix the bytes or the math.

✓ Roofline: two numbers

Intensity against the ridge tells you which resource is the limit and how far you are from the roof. Below the ridge, cut the bytes or batch more tokens. Above it, cut the FLOPs or get more compute.

Is it the whole truth?

No. It's a first-order model, and it predicts a floor, not a measurement:

  • Real kernels don't reach the roof. The Scaling Book says H100s and B200s usually get about 80–85% of their spec FLOP/s (TPUs get closer to 95%). Bandwidth falls short too: your M3's measured decode implies about 51 of its ~100 GB/s (see prefill vs decode).
  • Caches bend the byte count. Data that stays in L2 or shared memory (50 MB and 256 KB per SM on an H100) doesn't cost HBM bandwidth. Tiling a big matmul re-reads chunks, which adds bytes. Either way, the effective bandwidth ends up higher or lower than the spec.
  • Not every operation runs on the Tensor Cores. A vector add runs on the CUDA cores, which peak at about 66 TF/s on an H100 against the Tensor Cores' 990 (Scaling Book Part 12). Their ridge is about 66e12 ÷ 3.35e12 ≈ 20, not 295. At I = 0.167, the vector add is still far below it.
  • Tiny operations are dominated by overhead. A 1.79 µs vector add is short enough that kernel launch and synchronisation costs, which the roofline doesn't model, can matter as much as the bytes.
  • Other roofs exist. Across several chips, the bottleneck can be the interconnect, with its own and much lower ridge. At long context, decode also reads the KV cache (1.07 GB for an 8k-token sequence), and attention's intensity doesn't grow with batch size.

For the rigorous version (T_math vs T_comms, lower and upper bounds, and the critical batch size for a whole model), see roofline and critical batch size in perf-2.
