Study plan · Day 3

The math of serving

Day 3 of the inference study plan: the step-time formula, why memory decides the batch, and how one chip becomes a fleet. It covers all of perf-2 and the Scaling Book inference chapter, checks it with Serving station 2, and ends with the Serving guide's capacity-calculator project.

By the end of all of this, you'll be able to
  • Explain why caching K and V is exact, and why a 183× cut in FLOPs becomes only a 3.6× cut in time.
  • Compute any operation's arithmetic intensity, compare it with the chip's ridge point, and derive the critical batch size.
  • Fill in the step-time formula for any model, chip, batch and context, and predict where throughput flattens.
  • Count a model's parameters and KV bytes from its shapes, and work out how many sequences fit on given hardware.
  • Explain why throughput ∝ 1 ÷ KV bytes per token once memory is full, and rank the tricks that shrink the KV cache.
  • Choose a sharding for prefill and for generation, and size a Llama-3-70B deployment end to end.
  • Build a capacity calculator on paper and check it against a real server's numbers.
The same routine every phase
  1. Prime (5–10 min): skim headings and each card's Prime box, nothing else.
  2. Pretest: try the phase's checkpoint cold. Getting it wrong tells your brain what to look for.
  3. Read in order, and do each card's Retrieve questions with the card closed.
  4. Take the checkpoint for real. Open “Go deeper” only for cards whose questions you missed.
