Reasoning study plan · Day 1

Just enough about the model

Day 1 of the reasoning-models path: what training actually does to a model, how text becomes a next-token guess, and the handful of upgrades that separate today's models from the 2017 transformer. No prior deep-learning experience assumed.

By the end of all of this, you'll be able to
  • Say in two sentences what one training step does to a model's numbers, and what the loss measures.
  • Follow one piece of text through tokens, embeddings and attention to a probability for the next token.
  • Explain why the same prompt can give different answers (sampling), which later days depend on.
  • Name what RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU and grouped-query attention each replaced, and why a mixture-of-experts model runs only part of itself per token.
The same routine every phase
  1. Prime (5 min): read the phase's questions below its intro. Nothing else yet.
  2. Pretest: where a phase has a checkpoint, try it cold, without opening the answers.
  3. Read in order, with the questions in mind. The phase opens with a card from this hub; the readings after it open in new tabs.
  4. Take the checkpoint for real. For each miss, reopen only the source its answer points to.

Phase 1 · Learning is nudging numbers

~1 h · 0 / 3

A model is a big function with millions of adjustable numbers. Training measures how wrong it is and nudges every number a little in the direction that makes it less wrong. Everything later in the path is a different answer to “wrong compared with what?”.

Phase 2 · Text in, next token out

~1.5 h · 0 / 5

A language model only ever does one thing: given some tokens, give a probability for every possible next token. This phase follows text through that pipeline once.

  1. Why tokenizationWhat the model actually sees instead of letters, and why that shapes what it's good at.
  2. What we're building: a character-level GPTNext-token prediction in its smallest form. Every reasoning model is still this underneath.
    Go deeper (1)
    • Models station 3: the transformer ↗
  3. Embeddings and positions skimHow a token id becomes a vector, and how the model knows word order.
  4. One self-attention headHow each token looks back at the earlier ones. This is what makes written-out reasoning useful: later tokens can read the steps.
  5. Nucleus sampling (top-p)Why the same question gives different answers. Days 3 and 4 depend on sampling many different answers.
