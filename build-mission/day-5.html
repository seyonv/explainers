Make it useful

Day 5 of the Build an LLM path: Raschka chapters 6 and 7 and appendix E. Fine-tune your pretrained model twice, into a classifier and then into an assistant that follows instructions, score it with another LLM, and do the same more cheaply with LoRA. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the Classic version of this day.

The mission Find out how much domain text a tiny GPT trained from scratch needs before it beats fine-tuned GPT-2, measured in bits per byte on weather-service forecast discussions.

What today uses, and why

A pretrained model only continues text. Today you fine-tune it to classify, then to follow instructions, then do it cheaply with LoRA.

Raschka chapters 6 and 7, appendix EFine-tuning.
The two kinds of fine-tuning, an LLM judge, and LoRA.
Two cards written for this pathInstruction tuning and LoRA.
The ideas behind the code, measured on a real model.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Explain the difference between classification fine-tuning and instruction fine-tuning.
  • Format instruction data, batch it with padding, and mask the loss so only the answer is graded.
  • Score a fine-tuned model's answers with a local judge model, and name two ways that can mislead.
  • Explain LoRA: what is frozen, what is trained, and why it saves memory.

Task01 / 11

Make the quiz from your best sweep model

Work through it actively. Don't just read it. The sweep saved every from-scratch model; --crossover names the best one. quiz.txt mixes 10 real excerpts from the test file with 10 generated ones, with the title and any line that names a year removed from both; answers.txt says which is which.

python3 sweep.py --crossover | tail -1
uv run --python 3.12 --with numpy --with torch python sample.py models/<the file it names> --pairs 10

Work through it actively. Don't just read it. Don't tell them how many are fake. Fooled = a generated excerpt called real, or a real one called generated, out of all guesses.
