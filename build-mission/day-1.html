Build an LLM · mission · Day 1 · one page

Tools, and what training does

Day 1 of the Build an LLM path: just enough PyTorch to read and write the book's code, and what “training” actually does to a model's numbers, built up from one neuron. You need your copy of Raschka's Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch) and Python with torch installed. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the Classic version of this day.

The mission Find out how much domain text a tiny GPT trained from scratch needs before it beats fine-tuned GPT-2, measured in bits per byte on weather-service forecast discussions.
Today's move A byte bigram on your corpus: what one byte of context buys
Rung 0 of 11

What today uses, and why

LLM basics showed you what a model does. This path has you build one. Day 1 gives you the two things the book assumes: PyTorch, and a feel for what training changes.

Raschka appendix AThe book's PyTorch introduction.
Everything later is written in it. Typing it out is faster than looking things up later.
Zero to Hero courses 2 and 3Micrograd and the bigram model.
The book lets PyTorch compute gradients. Doing it by hand once makes loss.backward() something you understand rather than trust.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Create tensors, run a small network, and write a PyTorch training loop with a data loader, without looking anything up.
  • Explain what a gradient is and what one optimizer step does to a weight, with numbers.
  • Say why next-token prediction is trained with cross-entropy, and what its value means.
  • Train a tiny language model (the bigram) yourself and sample from it.

Today's move · A byte bigram on your corpus

~30 min · 0 / 2

The Zero to Hero bigram (Phase 3) predicts names from one character back. bpb.py's bigram is the same model over bytes of forecast text, scored the way every later model is.

Task01 / 12

Train it on 1 MB and on 3 MB

Work through it actively. Don't just read it. 

python3 bpb.py --bigram --mb 1
python3 bpb.py --bigram --mb 3

You should see 3.688 at 1 MB and 3.684 at 3 MB: more text barely helps a model with one byte of memory.

Task02 / 12

By hand: why does bigram_bpb add k to every count?

Work through it actively. Don't just read it. Open bpb.py. What would a byte pair that never appeared in training cost, in bits, without it? Then check which k it picked, and on which file.

You should see without smoothing an unseen pair has probability 0, which costs infinitely many bits; k is chosen on the validation file, never on test.
