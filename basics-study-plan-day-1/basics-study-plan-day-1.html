LLM basics study plan · Day 1

Text → vectors

Day 1 of the LLM basics path: what happens to your text before the model does any thinking. It's cut into tokens, each token becomes an integer, and each integer becomes a vector. You follow one question, “What is the capital of India?”, through a real model the whole way.

By the end of all of this, you'll be able to
  • Trace the whole route from text to answer once, and say the shape of the data at each stop.
  • Explain why models read tokens rather than letters or words, and how byte-pair encoding learns its pieces.
  • Say what a token ID is, and why it has to become a vector before the model can use it.
  • Explain why related tokens end up close together in embedding space, and what that does and doesn't tell you.
The same routine every phase
  1. Prime (5 min): read the phase's intro and each card's green Prime box. Nothing else yet.
  2. Read in order. Cards first, then the paper with its listed sections only.
  3. Say it back at the end of each day, closed book, before checking.

Phase 2 · Text → tokens → IDs

~1.5 h · 0 / 5

The model never sees letters. A tokenizer cuts text into pieces learned from data and gives each piece a number.

  1. Why tokenizationWhy not letters, why not whole words, and which odd behaviours tokenization explains.
  2. BPE by handByte-pair encoding worked by hand: merge the most frequent pair, repeat.
  3. What tokens look like skimReal text cut up: numbers, capitals, other languages, code.

Phase 3 · IDs → vectors

~1.25 h · 0 / 3

An ID is just a label. The model looks up a learned vector for it, and training arranges those vectors so similar tokens sit near each other.

  1. Similar words end up close togetherWritten for this path. The lookup, cosine similarity, word2vec's idea, and what Qwen's own table looks like.
  2. Token and position embeddings skimThe same lookup in nanoGPT's code, and a first look at positions (tomorrow's first stop).
    Go deeper (1)
    • Bengio 2003: characters as points in space · where learned embeddings started
  3. Paper: Efficient Estimation of Word Representations (word2vec) ↗ skimMikolov et al. 2013. Read the abstract, the two model figures (CBOW and skip-gram) and the analogy examples.
    Go deeper (1)
    • Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases ↗ · the follow-up: negative sampling and phrases
