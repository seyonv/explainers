Setting up your M3 for the course

Also called: the course environment, the venv. No lecture of its own; the same device check appears in Lecture 10 (GPT-2) · sample, auto-detect the device · 0:41:47

Prime · 2 minutes before you read

The problem: Every card in this series says "you should see" and prints exact numbers. If your Python, torch version, device or random generator differs from the one those numbers came from, you can't tell a bug in your code from a difference in your setup.

Where it fits: This is the ground floor of the toolkit, next to the Python card. Every later lab assumes this environment, and the cpu-vs-mps choice made here comes back in the GPT and GPT-2 lectures.

In one breath: one isolated Python 3.10 environment with torch, numpy, matplotlib, jupyter and tiktoken, Karpathy's repos next to it, and the two datasets on disk. Then a two-second script proves it works. Inside that environment, you run the small makemore nets on device='cpu' and the GPT lectures on 'mps', the M3's GPU.

Predict first: seed a generator with 2147483647 and draw torch.rand(3). The L2 notebook got [0.7081, 0.3542, 0.1054]. Do you get the same numbers if the generator lives on mps instead of the CPU?

No. The CPU generator gives [0.7081, 0.3542, 0.1054], digit for digit the notebook's. An mps generator with the same seed gives [0.9843, 0.4575, 0.7541] (measured on your M3, torch 2.14). A seed fixes the stream of one random-number generator. A different device's generator runs a different stream. This is why the makemore lectures stay on the CPU.

What goes where

Terms: a venv (virtual environment) is a folder holding its own Python and packages, so nothing clashes with other projects. MPS (Metal Performance Shaders) is PyTorch's backend for the Apple GPU. CUDA is NVIDIA's; your Mac doesn't have it. A seed is the starting number of a pseudo-random generator: same seed, same generator, same draws.

Why it beats the alternatives

✗ Use mps for everything

The GPU generator runs a different random stream ([0.9843, …] instead of [0.7081, …]), so your makemore losses and samples stop lining up with the notebooks you're checking against.

✗ Only Google Colab

No install, and Karpathy links a Colab for most lectures. But it's a hosted notebook, not your own files and editor, and nothing in this series was timed there.

✗ Rent a GPU from day one

Only one run in the whole course needs it: GPT-2's 10B tokens. Everything else measured here fit on the M3, the longest in 1052 s, about 17.5 minutes.

✓ Your M3, cpu by default, mps for GPTs

Every lab runs locally, seeded numbers match the notebooks where torch allows, and the GPU is there when the models get big.

Will my numbers match the video?

Not always, even on the CPU. The seed and generator pin down the stream, but how each torch function consumes that stream can change between versions. On torch 2.14, torch.rand and the L2 multinomial draws reproduce the notebooks exactly. The bigram name samples don't: the video samples mor., axx., …, and your M3 samples cexze., momasurailezitynn., … Each card shows what your machine printed and flags where it differs.

The M3 is the wrong tool for GPT-2's full pretraining run, and it's memory-bound for big batches: 24 GB made B=4, T=1024 the practical ceiling in this series' GPT-2 test. For that lecture, follow along here and rent the GPU only for the final run.

5 · Predict commit to an answer before revealing

You draw torch.rand(3) with the CPU generator (seed 2147483647) and then move the result to the GPU with .to('mps'). Commit first: do the values match the notebook's [0.7081, 0.3542, 0.1054]? And what happens if you pass the CPU generator straight to torch.rand(3, generator=g, device='mps')?

Reveal

The moved tensor matches exactly: [0.7081, 0.3542, 0.1054], because the numbers were drawn on the CPU and only copied. Passing the CPU generator with device='mps' raises RuntimeError: Expected a 'mps' device type for generator but found 'cpu' (both measured on your M3, torch 2.14). So one way to get notebook-identical random init for a model on mps is to draw on the CPU and then move it.
