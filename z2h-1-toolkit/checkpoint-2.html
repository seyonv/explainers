Checkpoint 2: from counts and shapes to one loss number

Ties together tensors, dims and indexing, broadcasting, matmul by shapes, distributions and sampling, mean, variance and the Gaussian and softmax and cross-entropy: the tensor and probability toolkit behind makemore (Lectures 2–5).

What this ties together, and why now. From Lecture 2 on, every model runs the same short pipeline: look up or matmul to get scores, normalise them into a distribution, then either sample from it or score the right answer with −log p. Six cards each gave you one piece: which dim an operation walks along, how two shapes stretch to fit, how a layer becomes one matmul, what a distribution is and how to draw from it, why sums of random numbers grow like √n, and how logits become one loss. They go wrong together, too: a missing keepdim, a weight scale off by √fan_in, or a softmax applied twice gives a wrong number with no error. Check that they now form one pipeline before Lecture 2 builds it for real.

Comes back in: this is the first course, so there's no earlier course to revisit yet; checkpoint 1 covers this course's calculus half. These ideas return in the broadcasting keepdim trap, likelihood and NLL, one-hot and a linear layer, embedding lookup and expected initial loss. Come back here after those cards and redo the questions.

How to use this page. Close the six cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before you open its Check. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you first have to work out which idea applies.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which idea?You draw 10 samples from p = [0.6064, 0.3033, 0.0903] with a seeded generator, and outcome 0 comes up only 3 times. Is your sampler broken? What would convince you either way?
    Check
    • probably not: 10 draws is a tiny sample. The chance of 3 or fewer 0s in 10 draws is 0.0502 (binomial)
    • the law of large numbers: frequencies approach p as n grows, and the noise shrinks like 1/√n
    • the test: draw 10,000. The same seed gives 0.6088 for outcome 0
    • also check replacement=True. Without it, a draw can never repeat an outcome, and 20 draws from 3 outcomes raises an error

    If you missed it, reopen distributions and sampling, "More samples, closer to p" and "The replacement trap".

  2. C has shape (27, 10) and X has shape (32, 3). Write the shapes of C[X], of C[X].view(-1, 30), and of that times W1 (30, 200) plus b1 (200,). How many parameters do W1 and b1 hold together?
    Check
    • C[X]: X.shape + C.shape[1:] = (32, 3) + (10,) = (32, 3, 10)
    • .view(-1, 30): (32, 30). It relabels the same storage, so nothing is copied
    • (32, 30) @ (30, 200) → (32, 200). Then + b1 (200,) broadcasts one bias per neuron down all 32 rows, and the result stays (32, 200)
    • 30 × 200 + 200 = 6,200 parameters

    If you missed it, reopen tensors, "Indexing with a tensor of indices: C[X]", and matmul by shapes, "More than two dimensions, and counting parameters".

  3. debugYour 27-class model has a 200-unit hidden layer h (std about 1) and output weights W2 = torch.randn(200, 27). At step 0, before any training, the loss prints 28.29. What should it be, what's wrong, and what's the fix?
    Check
    • expected: a model that knows nothing should be near uniform, so the loss should be about −log(1/27) = 3.2958
    • each logit sums 200 products of unit-variance numbers, so its std is about √200 = 14.14 (measured: 14.19)
    • softmax only cares about gaps, and gaps that large push it towards one-hot, so the model is confidently wrong on most examples
    • fix: scale W2 down. Dividing by √200 gives logit std 1.0031 and loss 3.7596; multiplying by 0.01 gives 3.3042, close to 3.2958 (all measured with seed 2147483647 on random targets)

    If you missed it, reopen mean, variance and the Gaussian, "So a layer's output grows by √fan_in", and softmax and cross-entropy, "Scale stretches it, shift does nothing".

  4. Logits [2.0, 1.0, 0.1], and the correct class is 1. Compute p1 and the cross-entropy. Then add 100 to every logit: what changes?
    Check
    • exp: [7.3891, 2.7183, 1.1052], sum 11.2125, so p1 = 2.7183 / 11.2125 = 0.2424
    • loss = −log 0.2424 = 1.4170, which is also log 11.2125 − z1 = 2.4170 − 1.0
    • +100 changes nothing: every exp gets the same factor e100, which cancels. Only the gaps between logits matter (and computing them naively would overflow, so subtract the max first)

    If you missed it, reopen softmax and cross-entropy, "Softmax, step by step" and "Cross-entropy: −log of the right answer's probability".

  5. which idea?You normalise the 27×27 bigram count table with P = N / N.sum(1). There's no error, but P[0].sum() is about 7. Which rule let this run, and what exactly got divided by what?
    Check
    • broadcasting: N.sum(1) has shape (27,), and rule 1 lines shapes up on the right and pads the missing dim on the left, so it becomes (1, 27): a row
    • that row is stretched down all 27 rows, so column j gets divided by the sum of row j
    • the table is square, so the shapes fit by accident and nothing warns you. On the real counts, row 0 sums to 7.02
    • fix: N.sum(1, keepdim=True) has shape (27, 1), which is stretched across instead, so each row is divided by its own total

    If you missed it, reopen broadcasting, "Worked: the keepdim bug on a 3×3 count table", and tensors, "Worked: dim is the one that disappears".

  6. You build lin = nn.Linear(6, 100) and write X @ lin.weight for X of shape (32, 6). It fails. Why, and what does PyTorch compute instead?
    Check
    • nn.Linear stores its weight as (fan_out, fan_in) = (100, 6), the reverse of Karpathy's hand-written (fan_in, fan_out)
    • (32, 6) @ (100, 6): the inner sizes 6 and 100 differ, so it fails with "mat1 and mat2 shapes cannot be multiplied (32x6 and 100x6)"
    • it computes X @ weight.T + bias → (32, 100). Whenever a shape error appears, write both shapes side by side and check that the inner two match

    If you missed it, reopen matmul by shapes, "Which way round is W?".

  7. which idea?Your bigram net computes F.one_hot(xs, 27).float() @ W over all 228,146 bigrams. A teammate replaces it with W[xs]. Do the logits change? What does each version cost?
    Check
    • no change: a one-hot row has a single 1, so the matmul just picks out that row of W. torch.equal on the two results is True (measured)
    • the one-hot version builds a (228146, 27) float tensor, 228,146 × 27 = 6,159,942 numbers (about 24.6 MB in float32), and multiplies mostly by 0
    • indexing looks the rows up directly. It needs int64 indices (torch.tensor, not torch.Tensor), and the result is a copy, not a view
    • it's the same idea as the embedding table C[X]

    If you missed it, reopen matmul by shapes, "Which way round is W?" (its last paragraph), and tensors, "Why it beats the alternatives".

  8. For v = [2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9], you work out std = 2.0 by hand, but v.std() prints 2.1381. Who's right, and when does the difference matter?
    Check
    • both: the squared distances from the mean 5 sum to 32. Divided by n = 8, that's variance 4 and std 2.0. Torch divides by n − 1 = 7 by default: 4.5714, std 2.1381
    • the n − 1 (Bessel's correction) estimates a large population's variance from a small sample
    • it matters for small batches (BatchNorm's batch statistics); pass unbiased=False for /n

    If you missed it, reopen mean, variance and the Gaussian, "Three numbers that describe a pile of numbers".

3 · Build: the bigram model both ways, without training

runs on your M3 · under 1 sWhat it reinforces: indexing and keepdim (tensors), dividing each row by its own total (broadcasting), one-hot @ W as a layer (matmul), sampling with a seeded generator (sampling), the weight scale at step 0 (the Gaussian), and softmax and cross-entropy on logits (softmax). The idea: a table of counts and a one-layer net whose logits are log-counts are the same model, so their losses and samples must agree exactly.

Use labs/data/names.txt (from the setup card):

  1. Data. Map '.' to 0 and a–z to 1–26. For every name, wrap it in '.' and collect each pair of neighbouring characters (a bigram) as xs (first) and ys (next), both int64 tensors.
  2. Counts. Fill a (27, 27) table N, then P = (N + 1) / (N + 1).sum(1, keepdim=True). Check every row sums to 1, and print row 0's sum without keepdim.
  3. Loss, counts way. -P[xs, ys].log().mean(): one indexed lookup, then logs, then the average.
  4. Loss, softmax way. W = (N + 1).log(), logits = F.one_hot(xs, 27).float() @ W, then F.cross_entropy(logits, ys). Also check torch.equal(logits, W[xs]) and that F.softmax(W, 1) matches P.
  5. Shift and scale. Rerun the loss with logits + 5, all-zero logits, and W = torch.randn(27, 27) (seed 2147483647).
  6. Sampling. Sample 5 names from P and 5 from softmax(W), each with a fresh seed-2147483647 generator, torch.multinomial(…, 1, replacement=True), stopping at '.'.
Done when: you get 228,146 bigrams and N[0, 1] = 4410; both losses print 2.4546; W[xs] equals the one-hot matmul; logits + 5 still gives 2.4546, all-zero logits give 3.2958 and random W gives 3.7590; and both samplers print the same five names.
