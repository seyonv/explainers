Study path · Day 8 · one page

Scale out: one model on many GPUs

Day 8 of the inference study plan: what to do when one GPU isn't enough. It starts with Part 4 of the request-scheduler course, then covers the Serving across GPUs course (splitting a model, splitting prefill from decode, mixture of experts) and Serving station 6. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

What today uses, and why

Everything so far ran on one GPU. Day 8 is what to do when one isn't enough: first squeeze the one you have, then split a model across many, split prefill from decode, and spread a mixture of experts.

Request scheduler course, Part 4Two cards: the throughput levers ranked, and splitting prefill from decode.
The gentle version of today's ideas, read first.
Serving across GPUs courseCourse 6 of the Fast LLM inference series: Megatron, DistServe, DeepSeek-V3, MegaScale-Infer.
The papers behind each split, walked section by section, ending with sizing a whole deployment.
Serving station 6: scaling outA field-guide lesson.
It checks the day against systems that run at scale.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Rank the ways to raise one GPU's throughput before adding GPUs.
  • Explain how GPUs exchange data during inference and what the connection between them limits.
  • Split a dense model across GPUs with tensor and pipeline parallelism, and say what each costs when serving.
  • Decide when to put prefill and decode on different GPUs, and what moving the KV cache between them costs.
  • Explain mixture-of-experts routing and expert parallelism, and why uneven load across experts hurts.
  • Size a deployment: how many GPUs, and which layout, for a model, its traffic and a latency target.
How to use this page

  4. Tick Done as you finish a step. Take a break after each checkpoint.

Phase 2 · Splitting a dense model

~1.5 h · 0 / 5

How GPUs talk, then the two ways to cut one model across them.

Before you read · 2 steps

1 · Prime · 2 min. Read this list of what each card will let you do. Don't open the cards yet.

  • How GPUs talk: collectives and interconnects. Estimate what one all-reduce costs for a given message size, GPU count and link, say whether it is latency- or bandwidth-bound, and explain why tensor parallelism stays inside one NVLink node.
  • Megatron I: splitting the MLP and attention. Explain why column-then-row needs exactly one all-reduce per block, slice a 70B layer for any TP degree, and say what limits TP for a GQA model.
  • Megatron II: embeddings, the fused loss, and what TP costs. Build a 70B decode step time for any TP degree and batch from its three terms, and explain why TP stops paying at one NVLink node even when the bandwidth math says it could go further.
  • Pipeline and data parallelism when serving. Predict batch-1 latency and big-batch throughput for a TP × PP × DP layout, and pick the layout for a given model size, node count and latency target.

Phase 3 · Prefill and decode on different GPUs

~1 h · 0 / 4

When splitting the two phases pays, and what it costs to move the KV cache.

Before you read · 2 steps

1 · Prime · 2 min. Read this list of what each card will let you do. Don't open the cards yet.

  • DistServe I: why prefill and decode want different GPUs. Compute goodput for a split fleet, explain why colocation is capped by the harder SLO, and use Eq 1–3 to say when tensor-style vs pipeline parallelism gives lower TTFT.
  • DistServe II: placement, KV transfer and results. Size a disaggregated fleet for a target rate, estimate the KV transfer per request over NVLink and IB, and explain why Alg 2 aligns pipeline stages across the two instances.
  • Disaggregation in production: Splitwise, Mooncake and Dynamo. Decide whether a given workload should be disaggregated, and if so explain which hardware, routing and transfer choices a production system makes and why.
