Study plan · Day 7

Cheaper decode: inside engines, and structured output

Day 7 of the inference study plan: what a real engine is made of, the two ways to make each decode step cheaper (fewer bytes, more tokens per step), and how an engine forces valid JSON. It covers the Inside inference engines course, the core of the Structured outputs course and Serving station 5, and ends with the Serving guide's prediction-ledger project.

By the end of all of this, you'll be able to
  • Name the parts of a real engine (vLLM, SGLang) and what each does on every step.
  • Explain how quantization for serving cuts the bytes read per step, and how to check what it costs in quality.
  • Explain speculative decoding: why its output is exact, when it helps, and why it fades at large batch sizes.
  • Explain how an engine forces valid JSON by masking tokens, what that costs in time, and why valid is not the same as correct.
  • Predict, then measure, four mechanisms on the lab GPU, keeping a ledger of every prediction.
The same routine every phase
  1. Prime (5–10 min): skim headings and each card's Prime box, nothing else.
  2. Pretest: try the phase's checkpoint cold. Getting it wrong tells your brain what to look for.
  3. Read in order, and do each card's Retrieve questions with the card closed.
  4. Take the checkpoint for real. Open “Go deeper” only for cards whose questions you missed.
