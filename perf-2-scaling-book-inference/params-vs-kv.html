Memory: parameters vs the KV cache

Also called the KV cache size formula, the HBM budget, or "how many users fit". This is Scaling Book Part 7, "What about memory?", in full: LLaMA 2-13B's parameter table, why training and inference memory differ, and 2·bytes·H·K·L·T. The book's numbers are LLaMA 2-13B; Llama-3.1-8B on an H100 and the fit tables are our recomputation.

In one breath

At inference you keep one copy of the weights, shared by every user, plus a KV cache for each sequence that grows with every token. For long contexts a handful of sequences outweigh the whole model, so KV memory, not the weights, caps your batch.

How it differs from related ideas

✗ Weights: shared, fixed

One copy serves every sequence in the batch, and its size doesn't change with users or context. 26 GB for LLaMA 2-13B is paid once. It decides whether the model fits, not how many users do.

✗ Activations: tiny at inference

No backward pass means nothing is checkpointed: ≈ 80 MB for an 8k prefill, and long prefills can be chunked. In training the same stuff is "several TBs".

✗ Optimizer state: training only

The fp32 optimizer state that brings training to ≈ 100 GB for 13B simply doesn't exist at inference. This is why a model trained on a big cluster can be served on far less memory.

✓ KV cache: per sequence, grows

Scales with batch × context: 2·bytes·H·K·L per token, for every token of every sequence. It is the binding constraint on batch size, and it's read every decode step too, so it drives latency as well.

Is KV always the bigger problem?

No. The weights still dominate when:

  • Contexts are short or the batch is small. At batch 1 with 8k, Llama-3.1-8B's KV is 1.07 GB next to 16.06 GB of weights. At 2k you need about 60 sequences before KV equals the weights.
  • The model is huge for the hardware. LLaMA 3-70B in bf16 (140 GB) doesn't fit on one H100 or even 8× v5e. The first job is fitting the weights, with quantization or sharding; only then does KV cap the batch.
  • K is small. MQA or aggressive GQA shrinks KV per token (see KV tricks), and int8/fp8 KV halves it.

Also, "free HBM" isn't all KV in practice. Engines reserve a set share of memory for the cache up front. Kiely's Inference Engineering (p. 139) configures TensorRT-LLM to give 80% of the remaining memory to KV: a B200 with 180 GB of VRAM, 100 GB used for weights and buffers, gets 64 GB of KV cache. The book's step-time and throughput tables use the same accounting as this card; see modelling LLaMA 2-13B.

A unit warning: the book mixes GB and GiB. Its LLaMA 2-13B table lists "6.7" KV and 26 of weights in a row labelled GiB, but both are GB (6.71e9 B = 6.25 GiB; 26.03e9 B = 24.24 GiB). So its batch-16 total of 133.2 "GiB" looks over the 128 GiB limit, yet it says it OOMs only beyond 16. In consistent bytes, 16 sequences use 124.2 GiB (fits) and 17 use 130.5 GiB (OOM), so the conclusion holds.
