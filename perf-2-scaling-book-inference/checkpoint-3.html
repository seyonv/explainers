Checkpoint 3: from one chip to a fleet

Ties together sharding prefill vs generation, sharding the KV cache, designing an inference engine, serving LLaMA 3-70B and speculative sampling: what to split across chips, what to keep still, how to schedule prefill against decode, and how to read the cost off it all.

How to use this page. Close the five cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before opening its Check. A wrong or shaky answer isn't a failure; it tells you exactly which section to reopen. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you have to work out which idea applies before you can answer.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which concept?A model trained with FSDP is deployed with the same sharding config on an 8×H100 node, and decode is several times slower than on one GPU. Which idea explains it, and what does the card's arithmetic give for Llama-3.1-8B?
    Check
    • FSDP gathers each layer's weights from the other chips before running it: in decode that is nearly the whole model every step
    • 16.06 GB × 7/8 over 450 GB/s ≈ 31.2 ms per token, vs 4.8 ms reading it from one GPU's own HBM
    • decode should move activations, not weights: use model (tensor) parallelism or separate replicas

    Missed it? Reopen sharding prefill vs generation, "Generation: three strategies are out".

  2. The book says generation can be model-sharded about 64 ways for F = 16,384 at batch 32 on v5e, far past training's 4–8. Derive the limit and say what it compares.
    Check
    • per chip: reading 1/Y of the weights takes 2DF/(Y·Whbm); moving activations takes 2BD/Wici
    • comms win once Y > F/(B·β), with β = Whbm/Wici ≈ 8: 16,384 / (32 · 8) = 64
    • training compares comms with math; decode compares them with the (much larger) weight read

    Missed it? Reopen sharding prefill vs generation, "How far can generation shard?".

  3. which concept?Decode at batch 16 is sharded 8 ways on v5e, and adding more chips cuts HBM time per chip but the collectives don't get any faster. Which idea explains it, and what is the test?
    Check
    • latency-bound collectives (Appendix C): each hop has a fixed ≈ 1 µs cost
    • at B = 16, D = 8,192 int8 the activations are 131 kB, under the 360 kB (8 × 45 kB) needed to be bandwidth-bound
    • rule: latency-bound once Y > BD/45,000 ≈ 2.9, and the ring's latency term grows with chip count

    Missed it? Reopen sharding the KV cache, "Appendix C".

  4. A model with 8 KV heads is served on 64 chips. Where does the KV cache go, what extra communication does that cost per attention layer, and why is it worth it?
    Check
    • 8 ways by heads (one head per chip on that axis) × 8 ways by batch: KV[2, BZ, S, KY, H], no copies
    • two AllToAlls: Q from head-sharded to batch-sharded, and the output back
    • they move small activations (131 kB at B = 16 with Llama dims) instead of stationary KV (537 MB per layer): 4,096× less

    Missed it? Reopen sharding the KV cache, "The price: two AllToAlls".

  5. which concept?Chat users on an interleaved server complain that the stream freezes whenever someone uploads a long document. Which idea explains it, how long is the freeze for an 8k prompt on Llama-3.1-8B at batch 32 with 4k contexts, and name two fixes.
    Check
    • interleaving runs prefill on the decode GPU, and prefill is compute-bound: 133 ms for 8k tokens
    • a decode step there is ≈ 9.9 ms, so every user freezes for ≈ 13.4 steps
    • fixes: disaggregate prefill onto its own servers (the lab's P99 gap 63.8 → 6.8 ms), or chunk the prefill so it rides along with decode steps

    Missed it? Reopen designing an inference engine, "Why the two phases fight".

  6. Why does the book's LLaMA 3-70B pipeline want about 3 prefill servers per generate server? Write the balance equation.
    Check
    • prefill rate P / Tprefill must equal the generate side's completion rate B·G / (Tstep · ndecode)
    • P / 0.91 s = 32·G / (0.019 s · 512) → P ≈ 2.99 G
    • long prompts (8k) and short answers (512) put most of the work in prefill

    Missed it? Reopen designing an inference engine, "Sizing a disaggregated fleet", or serving LLaMA 3-70B, "Prefill and the end-to-end pipeline".

  7. which concept?Speculative sampling gives 2.24× at batch 1 on Llama-3.1-8B with a 1B draft (α = 0.8, K = 4). The same setup at batch 256 is 0.70×. Which idea explains the flip, and at roughly what batch does the gain start to fade?
    Check
    • verifying multiplies tokens per step by K + 1 = 5; below Bcrit those extra tokens ride on the weight read for free
    • once B · 5 passes Bcrit ≈ 295, the verify step is compute-bound and every drafted token costs FLOPs
    • it starts fading around B ≈ 60 (2.12× at B = 64) and is a slowdown by 256

    Missed it? Reopen speculative sampling, the batch-vs-speculation table.

  8. For LLaMA 3-70B on TPU v5e at 8k context, int4 on a 2x4 gives 1.80 QPS per chip and bf16 on its minimum 4x4 gives 0.27. Which two choices produce that 6.7×?
    Check
    • quantization: 4× fewer weight bytes means the model fits on 4× fewer chips with the same 43-sequence batch (0.27 → 1.11)
    • a slice bigger than the minimum: the extra HBM holds KV, B rises 43 → 140 at the same ≈ 19 ms step (× 1.63)
    • QPS per chip = B / (Tstep × 512 × chips) is cost per query

    Missed it? Reopen serving LLaMA 3-70B, "Latency and cost at that batch".

3 · Project: design a 70B chat deployment on H100s

What it reinforces: the generation sharding limit (sharding prefill vs generation), where the KV cache lives (sharding the KV cache), the prefill : decode balance (engine design), the sizing sheet and QPS per GPU (serving LLaMA 3-70B) and when speculation pays (speculative sampling).

Design, on paper or in a short script, a deployment of LLaMA 3-70B (F = 28,672, 8 KV heads, 80 layers) for 8k-token prompts and 512-token answers, starting from the serving card's H100 panel: FP8 weights and KV on one 8×H100 node, B = 424 sequences at 8k, 25.0 ms per step, 4.14 QPS per GPU. Use 989 TFLOPS bf16 and 1,979 TFLOPS fp8 per GPU, 3.35 TB/s and β = 3.35e12 / 450e9 = 7.44.

  1. Sharding check. Is 8-way tensor parallelism inside the generation limit F/(B·β) at B = 424? At B = 67?
  2. KV layout. With 8 KV heads on 8 GPUs, do you need batch sharding and its AllToAlls?
  3. Latency. How long does one 512-token answer take at B = 424? What would a latency-first tier at small batch get per token?
  4. Prefill fleet. Estimate one 8k prefill on a separate 8-GPU node at 40% MFU in bf16, and in fp8. How many prefill nodes per decode node keep the decode node full?
  5. Speculation. Should the throughput tier use speculative sampling with K = 4? Should the latency tier?
  6. Honesty. Which of your numbers are rooflines, and what does the book say to expect in practice?
Done when: you have a one-page design with the TP degree and its Y* check, the KV layout, the per-answer latency, a prefill-to-decode node ratio for both prefill precisions, a yes/no on speculation per tier with a reason, and a note on the real-world haircut. Then compare with the check.
Check: the numbers (computed with the cards' formulas)
  • Y* = 28,672 / (424 × 7.44) ≈ 9.1, so 8-way sits just inside the limit at full batch; at B = 67, Y* ≈ 57, far inside.
  • 8 KV heads on 8 GPUs: one head each, figure (a) of the KV card: no batch sharding, no extra AllToAlls.
  • 512 steps × 25.0 ms ≈ 12.8 s per answer at B = 424. The panel's FP8 batch-1 floor on 8 GPUs is 2.6 ms per token.
  • Prefill 8k: 2 × 70e9 × 8,192 / (8 × 989e12 × 0.4) ≈ 0.36 s in bf16, ≈ 0.18 s in fp8. The decode node finishes 424 / (0.025 × 512) ≈ 33 sequences per second, so it needs 0.36 × 33 ≈ 12 bf16 prefill nodes, or ≈ 6 in fp8. With a batch this large, prefill becomes the bigger fleet.
  • Speculation: at B = 424, B × 5 = 2,120 ≫ 295, so the verify step is compute-bound: no. For a small-batch latency tier, yes, while B × (K + 1) stays under ≈ 295.
  • All of it is roofline: the book says real QPS is about half, and it assumes perfect overlap and no activation memory.
