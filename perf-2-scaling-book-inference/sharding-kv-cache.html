Sharding the KV cache

Also called batch-sharded attention, head-sharded KV, AllToAll attention, and (Appendix C) latency-bound collectives. This is Scaling Book Part 7, "Sharding the KV cache", plus Appendix C and a short look at Appendix B. The book's numbers are TPU v5e; the 8×H100 and Llama-3.1-8B numbers alongside are our recomputation.

In one breath

With GQA or MQA there are too few KV heads to split the cache across many chips by heads, so you split it by batch (each chip holds whole sequences) and pay two AllToAlls per layer to move the queries in and the outputs back. At small batch these collectives are so small that their fixed latency, not bandwidth, sets their cost.

Where the cache lives: heads first, then batch

The book's rule: "we almost always prefer to avoid replicating the cache, since it is the primary source of attention latency." So first Megatron-shard the KVs along the head dimension. "This is limited to K-way sharding", so when the model has few KV heads, shard heads as far as they go and then shard along the batch:

The price: two AllToAlls per attention layer

Everything else in the layer is model-sharded over heads, so Q comes out of WQ split by heads. To meet a batch-sharded cache it must be reshuffled so each chip gets all the heads it needs for its sequences, and the output must be reshuffled back. This is the book's figure from the Efficiently Scaling Transformer Inference (ESTI) paper, redrawn:

Grey dashed boxes are communication; green is attention reading the KV cache; grey-outlined boxes are weight matmuls. In (a), splitting WQ by heads also splits Q the same way the KV cache is split, so attention is purely local. The book's caption: "we need two extra AllToAlls to shift the activations from model sharding to batch sharding, so they can act on the KV caches."

The full algorithm, as the book writes it

Attention with model parallelism over both Y and Z. M = N/K is query heads per KV head. A subscript says which mesh axis a dimension is split over; {UYZ} means "unreduced": each chip holds a partial sum; *H means "contract over H". The book: "I apologize for using K for both the key tensor and the KV head dimension."

#LineWhat it does
1X[B, D] = …existing activations, unsharded from the previous layer

Grey rows are collectives; green rows touch the KV cache. The lab runs these twelve lines on a 2 × 2 mesh of numpy "devices" (B = 8, N = 8, K = 2) and asserts the result equals unsharded attention: max difference 5.6e-16.

The book's verdict: "The new comms are modestly expensive since they operate on our small activations, while in return we save a huge amount of memory bandwidth loading the KVs (which are stationary)." With Llama-3.1-8B dims, one layer at B = 16 and S = 8,192 (our recomputation):

Q moved by AllToAll 1 B·N·H·2 B = 16 · 32 · 128 · 2 = 131 kB KV that stays put B·S·2·K·H·2 = 16 · 8192 · 2 · 8 · 128 · 2 = 537 MB ratio = 4,096×

When batch is too small or context too long: shard the sequence

The book's third option: "If the batch size is too small, or the context is long, we can sequence shard the KV cache." Each chip holds a slice of S for every sequence. "First we need to AllGather the Q activations, and then accumulate the KVs in a similar fashion to Flash Attention": each chip returns its own running max, softmax denominator and unnormalised output, and they are combined exactly as Flash Attention combines tiles. The lab checks this too (S split 4 ways, max difference 4.6e-16). The cost is a collective to combine the partial results.

Think of a library split across 8 branches. Head sharding shelves by subject, batch sharding shelves by reader, and an AllToAll is the courier van that swaps requests between branches.

Appendix B, briefly: 2D weight-stationary sharding

With 1D (pure Megatron) sharding, the hidden F dimension is cut so finely at high chip counts that it can become "significantly smaller than E" (dmodel). On a higher-dimensional mesh, 2D weight-stationary sharding splits the weights over both F and E, with activations sharded over E: "We perform an AllGather on the (yz) axis before the first layer, then ReduceScatter on the (x) axis." For attention the same trick shards over nheads instead of the hidden dimension, which lets it scale further. The ESTI paper has the details; the book's worked problem Q7 does the math.

How it differs from related ideas

✗ Head sharding only

KV[2, B, S, KY, H] needs no extra comms (figure a), but stops at K ways. Llama-3.1-8B has K = 8; MQA has K = 1. More chips than KV heads means some chips duplicate a head.

✗ Replicating the KV cache

Copies waste memory, and every copy is read each step. In the book's 64-way example with K = 8, replicating over the other 8-way axis leaves each chip reading 8× more KV bytes than a fully sharded layout.

✗ Sequence sharding

Split S instead: AllGather Q, then combine partial softmaxes Flash-style (ring attention in prefill). It's the right answer when the batch is too small or the context is long, at the cost of a combining collective.

✓ Heads, then batch

KV[2, BZ, S, KY, H] is fully distributed with no copies. The price is two AllToAlls on small activations, 131 kB against 537 MB of stationary KV per layer in our Llama-dims example.

Does serving Llama-3.1-8B need any of this?

No. On one H100 the 16.06 GB of weights and up to ≈ 59 sequences of 8k-token KV fit on a single chip, so nothing is sharded. Even across an 8×H100 node, K = 8 KV heads split exactly 8 ways by heads, so figure (a) is enough.

It matters when:

  • The model spans many chips. The book's generation section can push model sharding to F/(B·β) = 16,384 / (32 · 8) = 64 ways and warns it "assumes we can fully shard our KV caches 64-ways which is difficult". Batch sharding is the answer to that. See sharding prefill vs generation and serving LLaMA 3-70B.
  • KV heads are few. MQA (K = 1) can't be head-sharded at all, and GQA from the KV tricks card makes K small on purpose.
  • The batch is small. Appendix C is why small-batch, multi-chip decode doesn't scale linearly: at B = 16 each collective costs at least its fixed latency, and on a ring that latency term (Tmin·|X|/2) grows with the chip count. Adding chips cuts HBM time per chip but not comms time.
