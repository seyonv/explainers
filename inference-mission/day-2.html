Inside the GPU and the engine loop

Day 2 of the inference study plan: what one decode step does to a GPU, how to measure it, and why a server batches. It covers perf-1 Foundations and Part 1 of the request-scheduler course, and closes each half with a Serving field-guide station. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the Classic version of this day.

The mission Show that a chat benchmark picks the wrong vLLM config for coding-agent traffic, and measure how much goodput stock flags recover on one GPU.
Today's move How many tokens of KV fit on one 80 GB GPU next to the weights

What today uses, and why

Day 1 told you that writing speed is capped by memory bandwidth. Day 2 shows why on a real chip, then why a server batches many users together to get past that cap.

perf-0: the mapThe start-here course of the Fast LLM inference series.
For orientation only: one map from GPU price to cost per token, so you know where today's two courses sit. You skim it, and keep its glossary open all day.
perf-1 FoundationsCourse 1 of the Fast LLM inference series.
What one token costs: the parameters, attention and FLOPs, then the GPU, the roofline and the serving metrics. Its one formula, step time = max(maths, memory), is what every later day builds on.
Request scheduler course, Part 1A course on how an engine decides which requests run at each step.
It explains batching, the biggest serving lever, as a chain: each kind of batching fixes the problem the previous one leaves.
Serving field guide: reference architecture, stations 1 and 3Field-guide lessons with read-with questions, sources and a closed-book check.
They check each half of the day against real systems (a production deployment and vLLM's engine), so what you learn isn't only theory.
LabsShort Python scripts, in the Go deeper lists.
Optional. Run one when a card's numbers feel abstract. Prefill vs decode on your Mac is the one most worth doing.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Count Llama-3.1-8B's 8.03B parameters from its config, and explain why every decode step reads all 16 GB of them.
  • Compute a small attention by hand, and explain why the number of KV heads, not query heads, sets the cache size.
  • Sketch an H100 from memory, and classify any operation as compute- or memory-bound from two numbers.
  • Measure prefill and decode on your own Mac, and back out the effective memory bandwidth.
  • Compute TTFT, TPOT, throughput and goodput for a request, and explain why goodput sets the cost.
  • Explain why batching is the biggest serving lever, what stops it scaling forever, and how static → dynamic → continuous batching → chunked prefill each fix the previous step's problem.

Phase 3 · Why and how a server batches

~2 h · 0 / 12

Part 1 of the scheduler course. Each batching card fixes the problem the previous one leaves.

You need this because the KV cache decides how many requests a server batches; it's today's move.

Before you read · 2 steps

1 · Prime · 2 min. Read this list of what each card will let you do. Don't open the cards yet.

  • The scheduler on one page. Place every card of the course on one causal map, and say which term of the step formula a given change attacks, and whether it moves throughput or only who waits.
  • The token loop: prefill, then decode. Price any request from its prompt and output lengths, say which phase dominates and why, and explain why one weight read per decode step is the opening that batching fills.
  • The KV cache: what it stores. Compute a model's KV size per token and how many sequences fit, explain why the cache both enables fast decode and caps the batch, and pick a lever to shrink it.
  • The bottlenecks. Name the phase, then the term: for any symptom, say which bottleneck binds, prove it with a number, and pick the fix and its trade-off.
  • Why batching works. Compute a batch's step time and throughput from the formula, explain the three things that stop batching from scaling forever, and say what batching costs each user.
  • Static batching and its padding tax. Compute a static batch's padding and idle-seat waste from its lengths, explain why its TTFT is bad even at light load, and say when static batching is still the right tool.
  • Dynamic batching. Predict a dynamic batcher's batch size and added latency from λ and d, explain why it works for one-pass models and fails for generation, and recognise its idea inside continuous batching.
  • Continuous batching. Hand-trace continuous vs static batching on a slot grid, explain why a ragged batch is possible, and name the three problems re-deciding every step creates.
  • Chunked prefill and the token budget. Pick a token budget from a TPOT SLO with the formula, predict what a smaller or larger budget does to ITL and TTFT, and explain why chunking needs no prefill/decode flag.
