Bond pricing and duration

Also called present-value pricing, Macaulay duration, modified duration, DV01 and convexity. The source guide lists Financial Mathematics with no page, so this card is supplemented from standard fixed-income references. Running example: a 5-year bond paying a 4% annual coupon, priced at a 5% yield.

In one breath

A bond's price is its future payments discounted at one rate, the yield. Duration is the payment-weighted average time until you get your money back, and it tells you how many percent the price drops when the yield rises by one percentage point. That straight-line estimate is a little too pessimistic; convexity corrects the curve.

Duration alone always underestimates the price: it overstates the loss when yields rise and understates the gain when they fall. At 1 bp the miss is 0.000012, which is why DV01 is quoted from duration; at 300 bp it is almost a full point.

The words, two examples each

Think of a bond as a stack of IOUs with different due dates. Price is what the stack is worth today; duration is where the stack balances on a timeline.

In Python

def cash_flows(face, coupon, years):
    """(t, cash) for t = 1..years; the last year repays face."""
    c = face * coupon
    return [(t, c + (face if t == years else 0))
            for t in range(1, years + 1)]

def price(flows, y):
    return sum(cf / (1 + y) ** t for t, cf in flows)

def macaulay(flows, y):
    p = price(flows, y)
    return sum(t * cf / (1 + y) ** t for t, cf in flows) / p

def modified(flows, y):
    return macaulay(flows, y) / (1 + y)

def convexity(flows, y):
    p = price(flows, y)
    s = sum(t * (t + 1) * cf / (1 + y) ** (t + 2)
            for t, cf in flows)
    return s / p

flows = cash_flows(100, 0.04, 5)

Why it beats the alternatives

✗ Just use maturity

"Both are 5-year bonds, so same risk." The zero moves 4.76% per 100 bp, the 4% coupon bond 4.40%. Maturity ignores that coupons return money early.

✗ Current yield

Coupon ÷ price = 4 / 95.6705 = 4.18%. It ignores the 4.33 pull back to face at maturity and says nothing about rate risk.

✗ Reprice every scenario

Exact for one bond, but it gives no single number to add across a portfolio or hedge with. You'd rerun it for every yield move.

✓ Duration + convexity

Two numbers that combine across bonds (a value-weighted average), give a hedge ratio from DV01, and land within 0.003 of the full repricing at ±100 bp.

Is duration always enough?

No. Both formulas assume every yield moves by the same amount and the cash flows are fixed. Real yield curves also twist (short rates up, long rates down), so desks add key-rate durations, one sensitivity per maturity bucket. For large moves, repricing beats the Taylor estimate: at +300 bp even convexity misses by 0.066.

Bonds whose cash flows change with rates break the formulas outright. A callable bond or a mortgage-backed security gets paid off early when rates fall, so its price curve can bend the wrong way (negative convexity). There you use effective duration: reprice with a rate model at y ± Δy and take the slope numerically.
