Ties together tanh and BatchNorm, atom by atom, Bessel's correction, the embedding gradient, cross-entropy in three lines and BatchNorm in one line, then train, with the BatchNorm lecture's BatchNorm forward, BatchNorm at test time, saturated tanh and the step-0 loss, the MLP lecture's embedding lookup, and Checkpoint 1's rules. Prime · why this checkpoint, why now

You've finished the lecture. The second half did two things with the rules from Checkpoint 1: it pushed them through tanh, BatchNorm and the embedding table until all 26 gradients were exact, and then it threw most of those lines away, deriving cross-entropy and BatchNorm once on paper so the whole backward pass fits on one screen. With that screen, loss.backward() is deleted and the net still trains to val ≈ 2.11. Next lecture, autograd comes back. Check now that you could still say what it computes.

  • tanh and BatchNorm, atom by atom → twelve lines from dh to dhprebn; bndiff and hprebn each need a second branch.
  • Bessel's correction → dividing by n − 1 fixes a 1/n shortfall; PyTorch's BatchNorm mixes n (train) and n − 1 (running variance).
  • The embedding gradient → matmul, view back, then scatter-add 96 rows into 27 with +=.
  • Cross-entropy in three lines → dlogits = (softmax − one-hot)/n: pull every wrong character down, push the right one up.
  • BatchNorm in one line → three roads from x to the loss, one broadcasted line, then 200,000 steps under torch.no_grad().

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which idea?You vectorise the embedding gradient as dC[Xb] += demb. It runs and the shapes fit, but cmp('C', …) prints approximate: False, maxdiff 0.0367, and only 12 of the 27 rows are wrong. Which idea explains which rows fail, and what do you write instead?
    Check
    • C[Xb] copies a row of C once per lookup; a forward copy is a backward sum, so row c of dC must add every demb row whose index was c
    • fancy-index += reads the old rows, adds, and writes back, so for a repeated index one write overwrites the others
    • the 12 wrong rows are the characters looked up more than once in this batch ('.' ×29, a ×12, …); the 9 used once are right and the 6 never used stay 0
    • fix: Karpathy's double loop, or torch.zeros_like(C).index_add_(0, Xb.view(-1), demb.view(-1, 10)), bit-exact

    Missed it? Reopen the embedding gradient, "The lookup: route each row home, and add", and the MLP lecture's embedding lookup, "If it's only indexing, how does C learn?".

  2. Row 0's correct character h has probability 0.0178. What is dlogits[0] * n at h, what does the whole row add up to, and what happens to this row's gradient if the model puts p = 1 on h?
    Check
    • at h: p − 1 = 0.0178 − 1 = −0.9822
    • every other entry is its own probability; those 26 add up to 1 − 0.0178 = 0.9822, so the row sums to 0 (the lab prints 0.0; the batch's largest row sum is 6.05e-9)
    • a pulley: wrong characters are pulled down in proportion to their probability, and the total pull equals the push on the right one
    • at p = 1 every entry is 0: that row stops training

    Missed it? Reopen cross-entropy in three lines, "Worked example: row 0, context "aad" → next character "h"" and "A pulley: the pulls and the push balance exactly".

  3. In the atomic BatchNorm lines, dbndiff needs +=, but dbnvar_inv and dbnmeani get a .sum(0) and no +=. What is the difference between the two cases, and what does cmp print for bndiff after only the first branch?
    Check
    • bndiff genuinely feeds two tensors (bnraw and bndiff2): two branches, so the gradients add with +=
    • bnvar_inv and bnmeani are (1, 64) and were broadcast over 32 rows: one use per neuron, but each value is copied, so its gradient is a sum over rows
    • after branch 1 only: exact: False, approximate: False, maxdiff 0.00119; after dbndiff += (2*bndiff) * dbndiff2, exact: True
    • writing = instead of += breaks bndiff, bnmeani and hprebn (maxdiffs up to 0.031)

    Missed it? Reopen tanh and BatchNorm, atom by atom, "One number, walked all the way back" and "Is this how you'd write BatchNorm's backward pass for real?", and sum ↔ broadcast, "The two rules on a toy".

  4. which idea?Your BatchNorm one-liner passes cmp against the notebook's forward pass. You then switch the forward to divide the variance by n, like nn.BatchNorm1d does in training, and the same one-liner fails with maxdiff 6.15e-5. Which idea explains it, and what is the fix?
    Check
    • the n/(n-1) in road ③ comes straight from the forward's ÷(n − 1) variance (Bessel's correction)
    • with a ÷n variance that factor is 1: replace n/(n-1) by 1 and the check passes (maxdiff 1.40e-9 in this page's build)
    • the one-liner is tied to its forward pass: derive it again if the forward changes, and at test time with fixed running statistics roads ② and ③ vanish

    Missed it? Reopen BatchNorm in one line, "The derivation, in the order the gradient flows" and "Should you train like this from now on?", and Bessel's correction, "Where BatchNorm mixes the two".

  5. debugYour no-autograd run (the 12,297-parameter net) prints loss 3.7954 at step 0, and your one-batch gradient check passed. But the minibatch loss is 3.7384 at step 1, 5.9133 at step 10 and 829.55 at step 100. What's wrong, and why didn't the step-0 loss warn you?
    Check
    • the loss is climbing: you're stepping uphill, so every update has the wrong sign (these numbers are from this page's scratch run with all seven gradients negated)
    • if the gradient check passed, the flip is in the update, e.g. p.data += lr * grad, which the check never sees; if it's in dlogits (one_hot − softmax), cmp on it prints approximate: False
    • the step-0 loss is computed before any update, so it tests the init and the forward pass, never the backward pass (compare 3.2958 for uniform guessing)
    • contrast: forgetting dlogits /= n makes every gradient 32× too big: 7.2583 at step 1 and 17.62 at step 100, then it lurches back down (2.3853 at step 1,000)

    Missed it? Reopen cross-entropy in three lines, "The derivation, in four lines", BatchNorm in one line, "Exercise 4: the whole backward pass on one screen", and the step-0 loss, "The starter MLP at step 0".

  6. Averaged over 10,000 batches of 32, the divide-by-n variance of one neuron comes out 0.9679 of the full-data truth. Yet in this page's build, calibrating BatchNorm on all 182,625 training examples gives val 2.197486 whether you divide by n or n − 1. Explain both.
    Check
    • a batch's own mean is the centre that fits that batch best, so squared distances from it come out low by a factor (n − 1)/n = 31/32 = 0.9688 on average
    • n − 1 corrects it: 0.9991 of the truth
    • with N = 182,625 the two divisors differ by about 0.0005%, far too little to move val at 6 decimals
    • so the mismatch lives in batch-of-32 statistics: PyTorch's train mode uses ÷32 (output std 1.0000 on the lab's batch) but stores the ÷31 running variance (0.9842 at test)

    Missed it? Reopen Bessel's correction, "Two ways to divide" and "The gap shrinks with batch size", and BatchNorm at test time, "Option 1: calibrate after training".

  7. Row 0 of neuron 0 has h = −0.499967 and dh = −0.00127939. Get dhpreact for this entry, say why the line uses h and not hpreact, and say what would happen to the same line for a unit with h = 0.995.
    Check
    • tanh′ = 1 − h² = 1 − 0.249967 = 0.750033; × −0.00127939 ≈ −0.00095958 (the lab's full-precision tensors give −0.000959586)
    • the output h is already stored, and tanh′ can be written in terms of it; using the input would recompute tanh
    • at h = 0.995 the slope is 1 − 0.990025 = 0.009975: about 1% of the gradient gets through, so a saturated unit barely learns (computed)

    Missed it? Reopen tanh and BatchNorm, atom by atom, "One number, walked all the way back", and saturated tanh, "On the way back, tanh is a gate".

  8. which idea?Two numbers look like bugs: every column of dhprebn sums to about 0 (at most 5.6e-9, against entries up to 0.0085), and db1 is at most about 2.3e-9. Which single idea explains both, and which term of the one-liner produces it?
    Check
    • BatchNorm subtracts each neuron's batch mean, so sliding all 32 inputs of a neuron up together leaves the standardised output, and the loss, unchanged
    • so the gradient has no component along "all rows equal": each column sums to 0, and db1 = dhprebn.sum(0) is that very column sum
    • in the one-liner, road ② (− dhpreact.sum(0), via μ) and road ③ remove it
    • that's why b1 is useless before BatchNorm; Karpathy keeps it only to practise its gradient

    Missed it? Reopen BatchNorm in one line, "One entry, through the one-liner", the embedding gradient, "Things that look like bugs but aren't", and BatchNorm forward's predict question about b1.

3 · Build: two copies of the net, autograd vs yours, side by side

runs on your M3 · about 9 s

What it reinforces: the three-line cross-entropy gradient, matmul and tanh lines, the BatchNorm one-liner, the embedding scatter-add (as index_add_), Bessel's correction both in the forward pass and in the one-liner's factor, and calibrating BatchNorm after training (BatchNorm at test time). New here: instead of checking one batch and then trusting your gradients for 200,000 steps, you run autograd alongside for the whole run and watch the two copies stay together.

  1. Two copies. init_params(n_hidden=200) from labs/common.py (12,297 parameters) is copy A; a detached clone is copy B.
  2. Your backward. One function: the Exercise 4 lines, with index_add_ for dC. The forward uses var(unbiased=True), as in the notebook.
  3. Step together. For 20,000 steps, draw one batch and update A with loss.backward() and B with your gradients under torch.no_grad(), lr 0.1, then 0.01 after step 10,000. At step 0, cmp all seven gradients; every step, track the largest |loss A − loss B|.
  4. Break the Bessel link. On one batch, redo the forward with unbiased=False and check the one-liner with n/(n-1) and with 1.
  5. Calibrate and score. Compute BatchNorm's mean and variance over all training examples, with n − 1 and with n, and print val loss for B (and A).
for i in range(STEPS):
    ix = torch.randint(0, Xtr.shape[0], (32,), generator=g)
    Xb, Yb = Xtr[ix], Ytr[ix]
    lossA = F.cross_entropy(forward(Xb, A)[-1], Yb)
    for p in A: p.grad = None
    lossA.backward()
    with torch.no_grad():
        cache = forward(Xb, B)
        lossB = F.cross_entropy(cache[-1], Yb)
        grads = backward(Xb, Yb, *cache, B)     # yours
        lr = 0.1 if i < STEPS // 2 else 0.01
        for p, q, gr in zip(A, B, grads):
            p.data += -lr * p.grad; q += -lr * gr
    worst = max(worst, abs(lossA.item() - lossB.item()))
Done when: both copies start at loss 3.7954; all seven step-0 checks print approximate: True (maxdiffs 2.8e-9 to 4.5e-8); over 20,000 steps the two losses never differ by more than 1.19e-6 and the parameters end within 4.53e-6; the biased-forward check fails with n/(n-1) (maxdiff 6.15e-5) and passes with 1 (1.40e-9); and val is 2.1975 for both copies with either divisor, the same val the batchnorm-analytic-and-train lab's --quick run prints. All measured in the checkpoint's scratch run on the M3 (8 s, other jobs running). For the full 200,000 steps, the lab reaches train 2.0712 / val 2.1086 in about 2 minutes.
