Roadmap · Hugging Face's Training Agents series

Training Agents: the roadmap

A route through Hugging Face's six-session Training Agents series for someone who has never run reinforcement learning. Each stage primes the ideas with drawings, points you at the right minutes of the right video, and hands you the actual scripts to run, cheapest first.

By the summit you can
  • Explain, with a drawing, what SFT, distillation, GRPO and environment RL each change in a model, and when you'd reach for each.
  • Turn a pile of agent traces into a LoRA fine-tune, track it in Trackio and evaluate it with Inspect, with a base-model comparison the class skipped.
  • Run off-policy and on-policy distillation from Qwen3-4B into Qwen3-0.6B and say why their loss curves can't be compared.
  • Write a verifiable reward, run GRPO, read the reward, KL and length curves, and spot reward hacking before the numbers fool you.
  • Build an OpenEnv environment with reset()/step(), push it to the Hub and train against it with TRL's GRPOTrainer.
  • Say whether a +5 point result is real: repeats, held-out splits and error bars.
  • Ship an original game as an RL environment, beat it with Q-learning, then train a small LLM to play it with GRPO, and defend the result.

The series in one picture

The four hands-on classes are one pipeline, each adding a stronger training signal to the same kind of small coding model. The two workshops are the frame around it: one on measuring agents, one on why training them with RL is hard. You'll watch them in the order the ideas are needed, not the order they were streamed. Around them: one hard-core RL day before anything else, and your own game at the end.

How to use this

Nine stages, about 60 hours in all if you do every lab, or roughly half that on the must-do path (see “How much to build” below). Stages 1–3 need no GPU. Stages 4–7 each have a free or near-free lab first, and the class's real HF Jobs command second.

You don't need to be good at maths. Every formula here is shown with small numbers you can check on paper. Where a class uses a word without explaining it (there are many: KL, entropy, LoRA rank, importance ratio), the stage's primer explains it before you watch.

Your own material is part of the route. Stages link to cards already in your hub (The Group Is the Baseline, the perf-7 training cards, the Codex pass@k cards) and to the Models and Agents field guides. Your mini-suey-rl repo is the bridge lab from toy GRPO to a real run.

The same loop on every stage
  1. Prime (5–10 min): read the stage's Prime box and try one checkpoint question cold. Being wrong first makes the reading stick.
  2. Primer: read each block, look at the drawing until you could redraw it, then open the “Best first” link. Tick the block.
  3. Watch in chunks: a chapter, then stop and do rough work until you have a question, then watch on. Use the timestamps; skim the parts marked “skim”. Treat a long video as something to absorb, not something to finish.
  4. Run it: do the labs in order. Cheap and local first; paid GPU only once the smoke test passes.
  5. Checkpoint: closed book. Missed a question? Reread only the block it names.

How much to build (and how much to just read)

The rule: build when the build answers a question you already have; read when you don't have the question yet. So each stage has exactly one must-build, under about an hour, that produces a number you predicted first. Everything else is optional play, done only if a checkpoint question went wrong.

Going fast without skimping means doing every must-build and every paper exercise (that's where understanding forms) and skipping the “skim” chapters and optional labs. The must path is about 30–35 hours plus stage 8's game. Doing everything is about 60.

Compute and money

Everything in the classes runs on Hugging Face Jobs, billed per minute from prepaid credits (a positive credit balance is the only requirement; PRO isn't). Your Mac handles the primers, the eval labs, OpenEnv's local server, every smoke test and all of stage 8's classical RL. Colab's free T4 covers the small SFT notebooks.

Known gotchas in the series materials

  • HuggingEnvs was renamed FineEnvs in September 2026. Old huggingface.co/HuggingEnvs/... and github.com/adithya-s-k/HuggingEnvs links redirect; these pages use the new names.
  • The series repo is burtenshaw/training-agents↗, but its tutorial README has broken video links (e.g. watch?v=distillGZT_oNQnLV4). Use the links on these pages.
  • J.J. Allaire is listed for the evals workshop but doesn't appear in the recording. The RL workshop recording starts partway through Lewis Tunstall's talk.
  • Class 1's final adapter and Class 3's k2/k8/k16 models are private (HTTP 401). You'll train your own anyway.
  • TRL's examples/scripts/gkd.py link in Class 2 is dead: GKD now lives in trl/experimental/gkd. Use the copy in the class's Hub repo.
  • Class 2 says on-policy distillation uses beta=1 (reverse KL) on air; the repo's command uses beta=0.5 (JSD). Stage 5 has you run both.
  • Class 4 quotes three different held-out results (0.49→0.59, 0.518→0.582, 0.541→0.635) from different eval sets. Stage 7 explains why that's normal, not suspicious.
  • Class 1 reports HumanEval 0.744 for the fine-tune but no base-model score, so there's no evidence the SFT helped. Stage 4's lab adds the missing baseline.
