Sampling temperature

Also: T, the optimal temperature T* · The paper tunes temperature per k. For a 679M model T* = 0.2 at pass@1 and T* = 0.8 at pass@100; Codex-S prefers T* = 0 at pass@1 and T* = 1 at pass@100 (Sections 3.3 and 4.5, Figures 5, 6 and 9).

In one breath

Temperature divides the model's scores for the next token before the softmax turns them into probabilities: low temperature sharpens the distribution toward the single favourite, high temperature flattens it so runner-up tokens actually get sampled. Because pass@k only asks whether any of k samples is correct, the best temperature rises with k.

Worked example: one choice between two tokens

The model is writing a loop body and has two candidates for the next token, with scores 1 point apart:

token A ("return") score 2.0 token B ("yield") score 1.0 p(A) = 1 / (1 + e^(-1/T)) since only the 1-point gap matters T = 0.2 e^(-1/0.2) = e^-5.0 = 0.00674 p(A) = 1/1.00674 = 0.993 T = 0.8 e^(-1/0.8) = e^-1.25 = 0.2865 p(A) = 1/1.2865 = 0.777

The two scores are illustrative; the probabilities and counts are computed from them (0.223 × 100 = 22.3 expected samples). The temperatures are the paper's own optima for a 679M-parameter Codex. Its reasoning: “higher temperatures are optimal for larger k, because the resulting set of samples has higher diversity, and the metric rewards only whether the model generates any correct solution”.

Why it beats the alternatives

✗ One fixed temperature for every k

Leaves points on the table at both ends. Figure 5's lower panel is the upper hull of the per-temperature curves, showing the optimum moving with k.

✗ Greedy decoding (T = 0)

Best for Codex-S at pass@1, and useless beyond it: every sample is identical, so pass@100 equals pass@1.

✗ Beam search

Returns high-likelihood variants of one idea. It does not give the independent draws the pass@k estimator assumes.

✓ Temperature tuned per k

Matches the decoding to the budget: sharp when you get one shot, diverse when unit tests will filter 100 samples.

Is a high temperature just better?

Only when something downstream can reject bad samples. At k = 1 the paper's own optimum is 0.2 (or 0 for Codex-S), because diversity with no filter is just noise. The numbers are also model-specific: Codex-S needs a higher T than Codex at every k > 1, which the paper attributes to Codex-S modelling a narrower distribution.

Temperature is not the only knob, and the paper does not tune them jointly. Sampling also uses nucleus sampling with top p = 0.95, so “T = 0.8” here always means 0.8 inside a 0.95 nucleus. The paper gives T* only for these models on HumanEval; it does not claim 0.2 and 0.8 are right in general.
