Learning to reason with RL

Day 4 of the reasoning-models path: training a model to reason instead of only prompting it to. Training on its own correct answers, GRPO with rewards a script can check, and how DeepSeek-R1 put it together. It follows station 8 of the Models field guide. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

Day 3 made a fixed model think better. Day 4 trains the model so that thinking well becomes its habit, which is what o1, R1 and their successors do.

Checkpoint05 / 10

Checkpoint: GRPO and rewards

Answer every question before opening its answer. Note which card each miss points to. From the Models guide's closed-book check for station 8. Take a break after.

  1. With a pass/fail reward, G = 8 and a per-sample success rate of 10%, what share of groups teach nothing, and which three changes lower it?

    Show the answer
    • p^G + (1 − p)^G = 0.1^8 + 0.9^8 ≈ 43% of groups have zero spread
    • Filter prompts to those solved 1 to 7 times out of 8 (DAPO's dynamic sampling)
    • Raise G: at G = 16 the share at p = 0.1 falls to about 19%
    • Order a curriculum from easier problems, and log frac_reward_zero_std to watch it

    Missed it? Models principle 12 · Models failure F16 · The group is the baseline.

  2. Your GSM8K reward gives full marks when the gold number appears anywhere in the completion. What will the policy learn, and what do you write before the first rollout?

    Show the answer
    • RL optimizes the check, not the task: listing several candidate numbers satisfies it without committing to one answer
    • Training reward climbs while strict held-out accuracy barely moves (F15)
    • Before any rollout, write the exploits as unit tests the reward must score 0: several numbers, the right number only in the reasoning, a final answer in the wrong format
    • Keep a strict extractor as the eval, and read random rollouts and track length as you train

    Missed it? Models failure F15 · Models principle 11 · Reward Hacking in Reinforcement Learning

Checkpoint09 / 10

Checkpoint: what RL does

Answer every question before opening its answer. Note which card each miss points to. From the Models guide's closed-book check for station 8.

  1. DeepSeekMath found RL raised Maj@K but not Pass@K, and Yue et al. found base models ahead at large k. What is RL doing to the output distribution, and what should you measure before RL because of it?

    Show the answer
    • RL sharpens the distribution toward answers the model could already produce, rather than adding new ones
    • So pass@1 rises while pass@k at large k may not
    • Measure pass@1 through pass@64 of the starting model first; the gap is the honest headroom (principle 13)
    • A problem at 0 of 64 gives RL nothing to reinforce

    Missed it? DeepSeekMath: Pushing the Limits of Mathematical Reasoning in Open Language Models · Models principle 13 · Models principle 9

  2. TRL's GRPO defaults to beta 0.0, no KL term. What does dropping the KL penalty buy, what risk does it add, and what takes over its safety role in your runs?

    Show the answer
    • It buys no reference model in memory or compute, and a policy free to move further toward rewarded behaviour
    • The risk: nothing in the loss pulls the policy back when it drifts, so collapse and reward hacking go unchecked there
    • Replace it with a strict verifier, per-step logs (entropy, length, clipped ratio) and held-out evals every 25-50 steps, keeping the best checkpoint
    • In Raschka's runs the variant with KL was the one that collapsed (33.4% to 0.4%), so KL alone is no guarantee

    Missed it? GRPO Trainer (TRL documentation) · Models failure F17
