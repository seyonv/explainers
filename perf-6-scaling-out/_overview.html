Choosing a parallelism layout

Overview of course 6, Serving across many GPUs, also called the scale-out map or picking TP, PP, EP and disaggregation. It ties together the section-by-section walks of Megatron-LM, DeepSeek-V3, DistServe and MegaScale-Infer, plus the cards on collectives, pipeline and data parallelism, mixture of experts, expert parallelism, production disaggregation and sizing. Paper numbers name their GPU and model. Llama-3.1-70B on H100 numbers are our recomputation from the course labs, with an illustrative all-reduce latency (1 µs per ring hop).

In one breath

Once a model doesn't fit one GPU, or one GPU can't serve enough users, you split it. Every split trades the memory and speed you gain against the time GPUs spend talking. Tensor parallelism splits every layer across GPUs. It cuts per-token latency but pays two all-reduces per layer, so it belongs inside one NVLink node. Pipeline parallelism hands each GPU a block of layers. It adds memory and throughput but not speed, which makes it the way to cross the slow link between nodes. Data parallelism just runs copies. Expert parallelism spreads a mixture-of-experts model's experts over many GPUs and sends each token to its own experts. Disaggregation goes further and puts prefill and decode, or attention and experts, on different GPUs so each can be sized and split its own way. The rule underneath all of it: NVLink inside a node is about 9× faster per GPU than InfiniBand between nodes, so keep the chatty splits inside a node.

The mental model: where each split lives

Chatty splits (TP, and the expert all-to-all where possible) stay inside the green NVLink box. Splits that talk once per layer block or once per request (PP, DP, prefill-to-decode KV transfer) are the ones that cross the grey InfiniBand link. MegaScale-Infer applies the same idea inside decode: attention GPUs and expert GPUs as separate pools.

These don't simply multiply. The DistServe and MegaScale-Infer multipliers are against 2023–24 baselines that lacked chunked prefill or large-scale EP. MoE's byte saving shrinks as batches grow, which is exactly why it needs expert parallelism. The all-reduce latency behind our TP numbers is illustrative, because NVIDIA doesn't publish one. The rented 8×H100 script on the sizing card measures it.

The words people use

All-reduce, all-to-all

All-reduce: every GPU ends with the sum of everyone's data (tensor parallelism uses it twice per layer). All-to-all: every GPU sends a different piece to every other (expert parallelism's dispatch and combine). nccl-tests reports busbw = algbw × 2(n−1)/n for all-reduce.

TP, PP, DP, EP

Tensor parallel splits each matrix; pipeline parallel splits the layer stack into stages; data parallel runs full copies; expert parallel puts whole experts on different GPUs. Written together as e.g. TP8PP2 or TP4×DP2, or DeepSeek's "attention DP80, MoE EP320".

Scale-up vs scale-out

Scale-up is NVLink/NVSwitch inside a node: 450 GB/s per H100 each way, marketed as "900 GB/s" bidirectional. Scale-out is InfiniBand or RoCE between nodes: a 400 Gb/s NIC is 50 GB/s. Bits vs bytes and one-way vs both-ways cause most spec confusion.

Active vs total parameters

An MoE model stores every expert but runs only k of them per token. DeepSeek-V3: 671B stored, 37B active. Memory and weight loading follow the total; compute per token follows the active count.

xPyD, goodput

Disaggregated deployments are written as x prefill workers and y decode workers (Dynamo, Kiely). DistServe's goodput is the request rate per GPU at which at least 90% of requests meet both the TTFT and TPOT SLOs.

Ping-pong pipeline, M2N

MegaScale-Infer splits a decode batch into m micro-batches that bounce between attention GPUs and expert GPUs so neither side waits. It needs m ≥ 2(1 + Tc/Tf), which is 3 or 4 in practice. M2N is its replacement for NCCL for this many-to-many traffic.

The takeaway

Use the fewest GPUs that hold the weights plus the KV you need, split inside a node, and replicate across nodes. Size by memory first: the model, plus KV for your concurrent users at their context length. Then use tensor parallelism up to the NVLink domain for latency, and copies for throughput. Reach for pipeline stages only when one copy needs more than a node. For big MoE models, pool attention with data parallelism, spread experts with expert parallelism, and keep a few redundant copies of the hot experts. Disaggregate prefill from decode only when traffic is large, prompts are long and the per-token SLO is strict.

Where this doesn't apply as-is: every H100 number here uses an illustrative 1 µs per ring hop, and the TP16-versus-TP8 verdict flips on it. Measure your cluster's all-reduce latency before choosing. The paper multipliers come from A100/H800/Ampere clusters against older baselines. Newer rack-scale systems (GB200 NVL72) put 72 GPUs in one NVLink domain, which moves the "stay inside a node" line. And a bigger tensor-parallel group means one failed GPU takes more of your capacity down with it.
