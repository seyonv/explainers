FlashAttention-2 I: fewer non-matmul FLOPs

Also called FA2, the un-scaled output trick, storing the logsumexp, or causal block skipping. This card walks Tri Dao's FlashAttention-2 (arXiv 2307.08691, July 2023): the abstract, §1, the §2 background and all of §3.1 (forward Algorithm 1, causal masking, backward Algorithm 2, MQA/GQA). The paper's numbers are on an A100. The operation counts, the Llama-3.1-8B block counts and the time estimates are our recomputation.

In one breath

An A100 does matmul 16× faster than any other arithmetic, so the few non-matmul operations in attention (max, exp, rescaling) cost far more time than their FLOP count suggests. FlashAttention-2 keeps the exact FlashAttention result but trims that work. It stops dividing the output by the softmax sum at every step and divides once at the end. It saves one number per row, the logsumexp L, instead of two, and it skips the blocks a causal mask would zero out.

Where FlashAttention left off (abstract, §1, §2)

FlashAttention already avoids writing the N × N matrix to HBM: it is 2–4× faster than standard attention and uses linear memory. But it reaches only 25–40% of the A100's peak FLOPs/s: 30–50% in the forward pass and 25–35% in the backward pass. An optimized GEMM reaches 80–90%. Profiling blames the gap on how work is split across thread blocks and warps, and the paper makes three changes:

#ChangeSectionCard
1Cut the non-matmul FLOPs without changing the output§3.1this card
2Parallelize over sequence length as well as batch × heads, to fill the SMs§3.2FA2 II
3Split Q instead of K across the warps in a block, so warps don't have to exchange results through shared memory§3.3FA2 II

Together, the paper reports about 2× over FlashAttention and 50–73% of A100 peak (forward up to 73%, backward up to 63%). In end-to-end GPT-style training it reaches 225 TFLOPs/s per A100, 72% model FLOPs utilization. The paper gives no ablation that credits each change separately.

The §2 background, in four facts. (1) A100: 40–80 GB of HBM at 1.5–2.0 TB/s, and 192 KB of on-chip SRAM on each of 108 SMs at roughly 19 TB/s. (2) A warp's 32 threads communicate through fast shuffle instructions. Warps in the same thread block communicate by writing to and reading from shared memory, which is the cost change 3 attacks. (3) The FlashAttention forward pass tiles Q, K and V and uses online softmax to rescale partial outputs, so S and P never reach HBM. (4) The backward pass recomputes S and P from the tiles. It has no rescaling but does 5 matmuls against the forward pass's 2, so it has to keep more values in SRAM, which is why it is harder to make fast.

Why non-matmul FLOPs matter (§3.1)

Tensor cores only do matmul. Everything else in attention (the row max, the subtraction, exp, the row sum and rescaling the output) runs on ordinary FP32 units. The A100 does 312 TFLOPs/s of FP16/BF16 matmul but only 19.5 TFLOPs/s of non-matmul FP32. In the paper's words, "each non-matmul FLOP is 16× more expensive than a matmul FLOP." A simple time model makes that concrete:

The rescale term is (ops per O element) × d / Bc, so it grows when the K/V block is narrower than the head dimension. At Bc = 64 and d = 128, FA1's rescale is 8 per score and FA2's is 4. The ceilings are for the arithmetic alone. Real kernels also lose time to memory, synchronization and low occupancy, which is why FA1 measured 25–40%, not 80%.

Algorithm 1: the loop order flips

FA2 also swaps the two loops. That is what lets Õ stay on chip, and it sets up the parallelism in the next card, where each thread block owns one row block of Q.

FlashAttention (FA1, Alg 1)

for each K_j, V_j block: # outer for each Q_i block: # inner load O_i, ℓ_i, m_i from HBM S = Q_i K_jᵀ m̃, P̃ = exp(S − m̃), ℓ̃ m_new, ℓ_new O_i = diag(ℓ_new)⁻¹(…) write O_i, ℓ_i, m_i to HBM

FlashAttention-2 (Alg 1)

for each Q_i block: # outer load Q_i; Õ_i = 0, ℓ_i = 0, m_i = −∞ for each K_j, V_j block: # inner S = Q_i K_jᵀ m_new = max(m_i, rowmax(S)) P̃ = exp(S − m_new); ℓ_i update Õ_i = diag(e^(m_i−m_new)) Õ_i + P̃ V_j O_i = diag(ℓ_i)⁻¹ Õ_i L_i = m_i + log ℓ_i write O_i, L_i to HBM once

In FA1, each (i, j) step reads Oi, ℓi and mi from HBM and writes them back. Our count from the lab: at N = 1,024 with 128-wide blocks (Tc = 8), O is touched 2·Tc·N·d = 2,097,152 elements in FA1 against 131,072 written once in FA2, 16× fewer. At N = 8,192 it would be 128× fewer. The paper frames the swap as enabling parallelism (§3.2), not as an IO saving. The IO view is our inference.

Causal masking: skip blocks, mask only the diagonal

An autoregressive model sets Sij = −∞ whenever key j comes after query i. Because FA works in tiles, (1) any tile that lies entirely above the diagonal can be skipped outright. That is about half the tiles for long sequences, and the paper measures about 1.7–1.8× over attention without a mask. (2) Tiles entirely below the diagonal need no mask at all. So with square tiles, only 1 tile per block-row gets the elementwise mask.

T = ⌈8192 / 128⌉ = 64 per side, 64² = 4,096 tiles computed = T(T+1)/2 = 64·65/2 = 2,080 (50.8%) masked = T, the diagonal = 64 (3.1% of computed) skipped = T(T−1)/2 = 64·63/2 = 2,016 saving = 4,096 / 2,080 = 1.97× → 2.00× as N grows (N = 131,072: 524,800 of 1,048,576)

Our recomputation. The measured 1.7–1.8× falls short of the 1.97× FLOP ratio. That fits the diagonal tiles doing full work for half the useful scores and the rows being unevenly loaded (row block i has i+1 tiles), but the paper doesn't break the gap down.

Scaled to the running example (our recomputation, A100 time model). Llama-3.1-8B prefill at N = 8,192, 32 layers × 32 query heads. The computed tiles hold 2,080 × 128² × 1,024 = 3.49 × 1010 scores:

matmul 3.49e10 × 512 / 312e12 = 57.3 ms FA1 other 3.49e10 × 8.08 / 19.5e12 = 14.5 ms → 71.7 ms FA2 other 3.49e10 × 6.04 / 19.5e12 = 10.8 ms → 68.1 ms (−3.6 ms, 5.1%) no skipping: all 4,096 tiles, mask on every one → 137.6 ms (2.0× slower)

Under this model, block skipping is worth far more than the rescale tweak, but both are free: neither changes the output. These are ideal-peak times for an A100, not a measurement, and they leave out the memory, occupancy and warp effects that the next card addresses.

Correctness, runtime, memory

Algorithm 1 returns exactly softmax(QKᵀ)V (no approximation), uses O(N²d) FLOPs and needs O(N) extra memory beyond inputs and outputs: just L. The paper omits the proof because it's "almost the same" as FlashAttention's Theorem 1. The lab checks it in float64. The max |O − Ostd| is 8.9e-15 for FA2 and 9.3e-15 for FA1, and FA2 differs from FA1 by 1.8e-15. The causal version is off by 8.9e-15, and L equals FA1's m + log ℓ to 7e-15.

Backward pass (Algorithm 2, §3.1.2)

This is nearly the FlashAttention backward pass, with one tweak: P is rebuilt from L alone. The outer loop runs over K/V blocks and the inner loop over Q blocks, so dKj and dVj accumulate on chip while dQi is read, updated and written back to HBM at every step.

D = rowsum(dO ∘ O) # once, before the loops (1 float per row) for each K_j, V_j: # outer; dK_j = dV_j = 0 on chip for each Q_i, O_i, dO_i, dQ_i, L_i, D_i: S = Q_i K_jᵀ # matmul 1: recompute S P = exp(S − L_i) # FA1: diag(ℓ)⁻¹ exp(S − m) → 3 ops; FA2 → 2 ops dV_j += Pᵀ dO_i # matmul 2 dP = dO_i V_jᵀ # matmul 3 dS = P ∘ (dP − D_i) dQ_i += dS K_j # matmul 4: read-modify-write in HBM dK_j += dSᵀ Q_i # matmul 5 write dK_j, dV_j

D is the rowsum(dP ∘ P) term from the softmax gradient (diag(p) − ppᵀ)dp, computed cheaply from O and dO instead of from P. In the lab, dQ, dK and dV match the textbook gradient to within 7.3e-14. The dQ read-modify-write is where FA2 II brings in atomic adds when it parallelizes this loop.

MQA and GQA: index, don't copy

In grouped-query attention, several query heads share one K/V head. Llama-3.1-8B has 32 query heads and 8 KV heads, so each K/V head serves 4 query heads. FA2 doesn't duplicate K and V to match the query heads. It computes the head index implicitly (query head h reads KV head h ÷ 4). In the backward pass, dK and dV must then be summed over the query heads that shared them. The lab runs 8 query heads on 2 KV heads: the summed dK matches an explicit-copy reference to 7.6e-14, and no K/V copy is ever made (4× less K/V memory than copying).

Why it beats the alternatives

✗ Rescale every step (FA1)

Dividing O by ℓ at every K/V block costs 4 ops per O element per step, 8.08 non-matmul ops per score in total. The answer is identical, and the extra ops cost 16× as much as matmul work.

✗ Store m and ℓ separately

That's two vectors of N per head in HBM, and the backward pass has to compute exp(S − m) / ℓ, 3 ops per score. L = m + log ℓ carries the same information in one vector and needs 2 ops.

✗ Causal: compute and mask every tile

At N = 8,192 that is all 4,096 tiles, each needing 16,384 mask ops, and 2,016 of them come out entirely −∞. That's about 2× the work. Our A100 model gives 137.6 ms against 68.1 ms.

✓ FA2: defer, compress, skip

An un-scaled Õ with one divide at the end (6.04 ops per score), one logsumexp per row, and only 2,080 tiles computed with 64 masked. The output is exact, and the non-matmul share of time falls from 20.2% to 15.9%.

Why do a few extra exponentials matter so much?

Because they run on a unit 16× (A100) to 500× (B200) slower than the tensor cores. On an A100, 8 non-matmul ops per score are 1.5% of the FLOPs but 20% of the ideal time. On an H100, the single exp per score costs half as much time as the 512 matmul FLOPs around it. On a B200 it costs as much as all of them. So from FA2 onward, each FlashAttention version is partly a fight against softmax. FA3 overlaps one warpgroup's softmax with another's matmuls ("pingpong"). FA4 rescales only when the max jumps by more than log₂256 = 8, and computes some exps with polynomials on the FMA units.

When it doesn't matter: in decode (one query token against a long KV cache) attention is memory-bound. Its intensity is about 4 FLOPs/byte on the running example, far below the H100's 295 (see attention intensity). There the time goes into reading the KV cache, and the non-matmul count barely shows. That regime needs the tools later in this course (paging, GQA, KV quantization) and decode-specific kernels such as FlashInfer's. The tweaks on this card pay off in prefill and training, where attention is compute-bound. Even there they are a modest part of FA2's ~2×. Most of it comes from parallelism and warp partitioning (FA2 II).
