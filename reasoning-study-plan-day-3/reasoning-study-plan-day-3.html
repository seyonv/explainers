Reasoning study plan · Day 3

Thinking at inference time

Day 3 of the reasoning-models path: getting better answers without changing any weights. Writing out steps, sampling many answers and voting, grading each step, and spending more compute where it helps. It follows station 7 of the Models field guide.

By the end of all of this, you'll be able to
  • Explain why writing out steps helps large models and not small ones.
  • Say when majority voting works and when you need a trained grader instead.
  • Compute a solution's score under a process reward model, and say why step grading picks right answers more often.
  • Choose between more samples, more revisions and a bigger model for an easy and a hard question.
  • Explain what appending “Wait” does in s1, and where the gains stop.

  3. Checkpoint: voting and verifiersFrom the Models guide's closed-book check for station 7. Take a break after.

    1. Majority voting and best-of-N with an exact verifier both use N samples. Why is the verifier version an upper bound on voting, and why does voting still matter?

      Show the answer
      • With an exact verifier, best-of-N succeeds whenever any sample is right: that is pass@N
      • Voting needs the right answer to be the most common one, so it can't exceed pass@N
      • Voting needs no verifier at test time, so it works where answers can't be checked
      • Report both: the gap between maj@N and pass@N is what a better selector could still capture

      Missed it? Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models · Models principle 13 · pass@k

    2. Best-of-N with a learned reward model improves from N = 1 to 4 and then gets worse. What pushes it down, and what would you use instead where the task allows?

      Show the answer
      • The pick drifts from the policy by KL = ln N − (N − 1)/N nats: 0.64 at N = 4, 1.84 at 16, 3.17 at 64
      • The further it drifts, the more the choice follows the proxy's errors, so verified accuracy falls while the proxy score keeps rising
      • Use an exact verifier or majority vote where you can, or rank by mean token log-probability
      • Otherwise choose N at the verifier's peak and report the KL next to the number

      Missed it? Models failure F18 · Mean log-probability ranking
