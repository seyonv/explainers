Checkpoint 3: when replicas must agree

Ties together what eventual consistency can't promise, invariant confluence, money transfer without distributed transactions, quorums and staleness, compaction, deletion and XA and consensus with Raft: which rules need coordination, and the cheapest way to pay for it where they do.

How to use this page. Close the six cards. Answer each question in writing, in full sentences, before opening its Check. A wrong or shaky answer isn't a failure; it tells you exactly which section to reopen. The questions are mixed on purpose, so you have to work out which idea applies before you can answer.

1 · Mixed retrieval

  1. which concept?A booking app replicates across two regions and lets each region confirm bookings locally. Both regions check for overlaps, yet a room ends up booked 10:00–11:00 and 10:30–11:30. Which test would have predicted this before launch, and what are your two options for this rule?
    Check
    • the I-confluence test: two locally valid states must always merge into a valid one; one counterexample (A 10–11, C 10:30–11:30) is enough to say No
    • option 1: coordinate this rule only, with one owner (or a consensus group) per room, so each room's bookings are checked in one place
    • option 2: reshape the rule so it passes, e.g. give each region its own slots or rooms to sell; everything else stays coordination-free

    Missed it? Reopen invariant confluence, the worked example and "Why it beats the alternatives".

  2. The transfer processor crashes after r2's debit and restarts from r2; later, a client retries r4 and someone replays the whole log twice. With dedup the total stays 150; without it, it drops to 130. Walk through where the naive version loses money, and why r3's outcome even changes.
    Check
    • restart: bob is debited for r2 twice (80 − 20 − 20 = 40), so 20 vanishes
    • the r4 retry pays carol 10 twice; the full replays apply everything again
    • r3 was rejected (carol had 20 < 25), but on replay carol has more, so the re-check passes: storing the result with the id is what keeps r3 rejected forever

    Missed it? Reopen money transfer, the hand trace and "Same events, without the seen set".

  3. which concept?Users of a store with n = 3, w = 1, r = 1 complain that right after saving their profile, the page often shows the old one. Which idea explains how often, and what are two ways to fix it?
    Check
    • partial quorums: w + r = 2 ≤ 3, so the read can miss the write; at the ack only k = 1 replica has it, so P(stale) = C(2, 1)/C(3, 1) = 2/3
    • fix 1: w = 2, r = 2 so every read set overlaps the write set (P(stale) = 0 at the ack)
    • fix 2: a session guarantee: route the user's reads to the replica that took their write (read-your-writes) and keep w = r = 1 for everyone else

    Missed it? Reopen quorums and staleness, worked example 2, and eventual consistency, "So is eventual consistency the wrong choice?".

  4. An XA coordinator logs COMMIT after both participants vote YES, then crashes for 30 s. What state are the participants in, why can't they act alone, and what does it cost at 1,000 transactions/s with an 8 ms round trip?
    Check
    • both are prepared and in doubt, holding their locks
    • having voted YES, they may not abort (the decision might be COMMIT) or commit (it might be ABORT); only the coordinator's log knows
    • 8 transactions are in doubt at the crash, and their locks are held 30 / 0.008 = 3,750× the normal hold

    Missed it? Reopen compaction and XA, part 2.

  5. which concept?In a 5-node group, the leader crashes. The node that is two entries behind happens to time out first and asks for votes. Can it win, and what guarantees your answer?
    Check
    • no: the up-to-date vote rule makes any server whose log is ahead refuse it
    • the last committed entry is on a majority (3 of 5); any 3 voters include one of them (2q − n = 1), and they all refuse
    • so only a candidate holding every committed entry can collect 3 votes; the lagging node gets 2 at best, and a later candidate wins

    Missed it? Reopen consensus with Raft, "Why S4 and S5 could never win".

  6. Ann and Bo both register the username sam on different replicas, both get "ok", and after syncing both replicas agree that sam belongs to two people. Which of the four target properties held, which one was lost, and why does converging not help?
    Check
    • held: transactional availability (both answered), convergence (A = B after the merge), coordination-freedom (each read only its own replica)
    • lost: global validity: the merged state breaks "one owner per name"
    • convergence promises the replicas agree, not that what they agree on is valid

    Missed it? Reopen eventual consistency, the worked example and the four properties table.

  7. An append-only store deletes user u1 by writing a tombstone. A compaction job that runs on the newest segment only drops the tombstone to save space. What happens next, and when is it actually safe to drop a tombstone?
    Check
    • the older segment still holds u1=ann@y; with no tombstone above it, that value becomes visible again: the delete is undone
    • a tombstone must outlive every older copy of the key, so it can go only when the oldest file holding u1 has been compacted too
    • and for true erasure, backups and replicas must be rewritten or destroyed as well

    Missed it? Reopen compaction and XA, part 1.

3 · Project: a prepaid credits ledger

What it reinforces: which rules break at merge time (eventual consistency), the per-rule test (I-confluence), a log with request-id dedup (money transfer), staleness numbers (quorums), why not XA and what deletion really takes (compaction and XA), and what the log's commit costs (Raft).

The image service sells prepaid credits: customers buy credits, every resize spends one, each customer picks a unique display handle, usage rows point at an account, and admins can delete accounts. Design it and build the core in about 50 lines of Python, starting from Account and process in code/exactly-once-transfer.py.

  1. Classify. For each (rule, operation): buy (balance ≥ 0), spend (balance ≥ 0), handle (unique, user-chosen), usage row insert (foreign key), account delete (foreign key). Mark each I-confluent or not, with a two-replica counterexample for every No.
  2. Build the spends. Put every spend and buy for an account through that account's partition of one log, each with a client-chosen request id. Replay the whole log twice and show the balances don't move. Then crash right after a spend is applied but before the processor saves its position, restart from the last saved position, and show the spend isn't applied twice.
  3. The dashboard read. The "credits left" figure is read from a 3-replica store. Compute P(stale) at the ack for (w, r) = (1, 1) and (2, 2), and say which one the dashboard can use and which one the spend check must not.
  4. The log's cost. The log runs on Raft. Compare a 3-node group in one region (both followers about 8 ms away) with the course's 5-node group led from East US (followers at 8, 69, 83 and 162 ms): commit latency, and how many sequential spends per second one account can make.
  5. Say no to XA. A teammate wants each spend and its resize job committed together with XA across the ledger database and the job queue. Give the monthly downtime of that with 99.9% parts, and the log-based alternative.
  6. Delete for real. An account asks to be erased. List what must happen to its tombstone, its older segments, its usage rows and its backups.
Done when: your classification table has five rows with a counterexample for every No, your program prints unchanged balances after two replays and after a crash-restart, and each of steps 3–6 has its number or list. Then compare with the check.
Check: the verdicts and numbers (computed with the course's code and the cards' formulas)
  • Buy: confluent (adds only). Spend: not (from 100, replicas spend 60 and 70 → −30). Handle: not (both pick "sam"). Usage insert: confluent (union never removes the account). Delete: not (delete bob on one replica, a usage row → bob on the other), unless the merge cascades the delete to the account's rows.
  • Spends through one partition per account in log order: the balance check is local and serial, so the non-confluent rule is enforced by a single owner. Buys only add. With dedup, two full replays and a crash-restart leave every balance unchanged; without it, the restart debits twice.
  • P(stale) at the ack: (1, 1) → C(2, 1)/C(3, 1) = 2/3; (2, 2) → 0. The dashboard can live with (1, 1) and a note that it lags. The spend check must not read a replica at all: it runs inside the account's own partition.
  • 3 nodes in one region: a majority is 2, so the leader waits for its fastest follower: 8 ms, 1000 / 8 = 125 sequential spends/s per account. 5 nodes across regions: the 2nd-fastest follower, 69 ms, 14.5 spends/s.
  • XA with the database, the queue and the coordinator all at 99.9%: 1 − 0.9993 → 129.5 min/month down, against 43.2 for one part, plus in-doubt locks whenever the coordinator restarts. Instead: commit the spend to the log once and derive the resize job from it, deduped by the same request id.
  • Erasure: keep the tombstone until every older segment holding the account is compacted; cascade the delete to its usage rows; rewrite or destroy backups (or destroy a per-user encryption key). A tombstone alone only hides the data.
