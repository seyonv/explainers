Reasoning study path · Day 1 · one page

Just enough about the model

Day 1 of the reasoning-models path: what training actually does to a model, how text becomes a next-token guess, and the handful of upgrades that separate today's models from the 2017 transformer. No prior deep-learning experience assumed. Everything is inline: just scroll. Ticks are shared with the checklist version.

What today uses, and why

Reasoning models are ordinary language models trained further. Before the reasoning methods make sense, you need the basics they all reuse: a loss, a gradient, next-token probabilities and sampling.

Today's models change four parts of the original transformer. Knowing which ones keeps papers from being confusing.

Why frontier models are huge but run only part of themselves, and how much data they're trained on.
By the end of today, you'll be able to
  • Say in two sentences what one training step does to a model's numbers, and what the loss measures.
  • Follow one piece of text through tokens, embeddings and attention to a probability for the next token.
  • Explain why the same prompt can give different answers (sampling), which later days depend on.
  • Name what RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU and grouped-query attention each replaced, and why a mixture-of-experts model runs only part of itself per token.

Phase 1 · Learning is nudging numbers

~1 h · 0 / 3

A model is a big function with millions of adjustable numbers. Training measures how wrong it is and nudges every number a little in the direction that makes it less wrong. Everything later in the path is a different answer to “wrong compared with what?”.
